# Surface combat spec

Status: implemented (`src/NavySimulator.Engine/Battles/`) and checked against **hoi4.exe 1.19.3** by static analysis. Evidence for every [RE] item is in [`docs/reverse-engineering/findings/`](../reverse-engineering/findings/README.md). Tags are explained in [README.md](README.md). Numbers in parentheses are vanilla 1.19.3 values; the engine reads all of them from `GameDefines`, so loading other defines (or a mod) changes them.

"Tick" is the game's combat hour counter. It is 1 during battle hour 0, 2 during hour 1, and so on.

## S-DESIGN: hulls, module slots and ship types [PDX + ASSUMED]

The game's ship designer rules, read from the script files and the game's own `common/units/equipment/_documentation.md`; none of it is decoded from hoi4.exe yet. Code: `DesignRules` (`Engine/Ships/`), checked by the loader (warnings) and the Designs page. Data: `hulls/`, `modules/` and `ship-types/` are written by `NavySimulator import-ship-data` (GameData `Import/`) from the game and mods, with the game's IDs.
- **S-DESIGN-HULL** [PDX]: a hull is a `ship_hull_*` equipment of a naval archetype (`ship_hull_cruiser`, `ship_hull_heavy`, ...). Its stats are the archetype's values overridden by its own [ASSUMED]: this reproduces all 27 hand-entered TFB hulls, while reading them along `parent` would give TFB's heavy hulls the pre-dreadnought's speed of 18 ([finding](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-STATS-design-stats.md), Unresolved). Equipment of the same archetypes without the `ship_hull_` prefix (`light_cruiser_1`, `battleship_1`, ...) belongs to the tech tree for players without Man the Guns (`technologies/naval.txt`) and is not imported. There is no battleship, battlecruiser, light or heavy cruiser hull: the modules decide (S-DESIGN-TYPE).
- **S-DESIGN-SLOT** [PDX]: `module_slots` lists the slots, each `required` or not, with its `allowed_module_categories`; a module fits a slot if its `category` is allowed, and a required slot must be filled. `module_slots = inherit` and `slot = inherit` take the parent equipment's slots (the archetype's when there is no `parent`); `slot = other_slot` copies an earlier slot. Slots named `fixed_*` are the designer's fixed slots, the rest custom slots.
- **S-DESIGN-FORBID** [PDX]: a module's `forbid_module_categories` excludes those categories from every other slot of the design, e.g. TFB's light cruiser armor forbids `ship_medium_battery` and vanilla's medium battery forbids light batteries.
- **S-DESIGN-LIMIT** [PDX]: `module_count_limit` counts the slots matching any of its conditions (`module`, `category`, `any`, `empty`) and requires `count < n`, `> n` or `= n`; limits are inherited along `parent`, a child's limit with the same conditions replaces the parent's, and `count = any` cancels it.
- **S-DESIGN-TYPE** [PDX + ASSUMED]: the ship type is a naval sub-unit of `common/units`. Candidates are the sub-units whose `need`/`need_equipment` names the hull's archetype; a candidate qualifies if the design has one of its `need_equipment_modules = { any = { ... } }` modules (when listed) and each of its `type`s is among the design's equipment types (the archetype's `type` plus every module's `add_equipment_type`); the highest `priority` wins. TFB tells light from heavy cruisers and battleships from battlecruisers by the armor module; vanilla makes a cruiser with a medium battery (`add_equipment_type = capital_ship`) a heavy cruiser by priority. The type gives the role (`screen_ship` → screen, `capital_ship` → capital, `carrier`, `submarine`) and the organization (`max_organisation`, S-STATS-SHIP). Technology (sub-unit) bonus filters match the ship type, the hull or the archetype ID; equipment bonuses and MIOs match the hull (S-MIO-MATCH). If no type qualifies (the game would refuse the design), the simulator uses the highest-priority candidate whose unit types match, else any candidate, and reports it.
- **Overrides**: a design with `allowAnyModule` may break S-DESIGN-SLOT/FORBID/LIMIT and the required slots; without it, the loader only warns. Modules in slots the hull does not have are an error.
- **Open**: whether the game's sub-unit pick works exactly so (the subset and priority rule is inferred from the scripts, not decoded); the hull stat inheritance (above).

## S-STATS: ship stats before combat [RE]

Finding: [S-STATS-design-stats.md](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-STATS-design-stats.md). Code: `ShipDesign.ComputeStats`, `Ship.ApplyExternalModifiers`.
- **S-STATS-DESIGN** (0xbdfc80): `variant = (hull base + Σ module add_stats + avg + MIO hit chance factors) × (1 + Σ module multiply_stats + MIO + equipment bonuses)`.
  - `avg = Σ add_average_stats / modules declaring the stat`.
  - All multipliers form one additive sum; nothing compounds.
  - Carrier size: `floor(add + avg + multiplier)` (its multiplier is a flat add).
- **S-STATS-MIO**: the MIO's `equipment_bonus` sum (S-MIO: initial trait, the side's unlocked traits that reach the design, the attached policy's matching blocks). Light, heavy and torpedo hit chance factors are added; every other stat joins the multiplier, so a percentage on a stat the design has 0 of does nothing. Code: `MioRules.EquipmentBonus`, passed to `ShipDesign.ComputeStats` per side.
- **S-STATS-BONUS**: idea and named equipment bonuses (`add_equipment_bonus`, e.g. TFB special projects) are multiplicative for every stat, hit chance factors included. In the data they are researches with `"equipmentBonus": true`. [RE] (0xc97e00) A bonus key matches a design as in S-MIO-MATCH: the hull, its archetype, the hull's type keywords or an equipment group holding one of them; a key naming a sub-unit (TFB's doctrine key `sh_carrier`) matches nothing.
- **S-STATS-SHIP** (0xc3d090): technology blocks are sub-unit bonuses. `ship = variant × (1 + tech) + tech` for the additive set: org, HP, reliability, hit chance factors, torpedo damage reduction, torpedo enemy crit chance and naval weather penalty. Organization comes from the sub-unit (the ship type's `max_organisation`, S-DESIGN-TYPE), so it is never scaled.
- **S-STATS-CLAMP**: reliability ≥ 0, production cost ≥ 0.1.
- **S-STATS-PARTS** [RE + CAL] (0xc3d340): a damaged part's `stat_penalties` multiply the stat by `max(0, 1 + count × penalty)` (count: how often that part is damaged; two damaged heavy gun instances at −0.5 leave 0). Every stat takes them. [CAL] In 11 replayed TFB battles: heavy guns ×0.5 and secondaries' light attack ×0.75 in every later shot; a short-circuited radar (`shortcircuited_radar_2`: surface detection −7, hit chance factors −0.025) takes its ship's surface detection to 0, which raised the enemy side's logged positioning by exactly that share of the detection difference (S-POS-DETECT) together with its screening and fleet AA, and lowers the ship's own light and heavy hit chance factors to 0.975 ×. (A first reading, "attacks only", came from part picks in the wrong slot order.) Code: `ShipStats.PartPenaltyBonus`, `Ship.DamagePart`.
- **S-STATS-COMBAT**: attack, armor and speed modifiers are combat-time, not design stats. The ship's own getters read the ship modifier sum `0xc38760` (country modifiers, night × darkness, the naval terrain's block (S-TERRAIN), the region's weather × the ship's weather factor (S-WEATHER), the admiral's modifiers (S-ADMIRAL-MOD), damaged parts' modifiers; `MemberModifierRules.ShipModifier`, `CombatStatRules`):
  - attack and armor (0xc34a50, [finding](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-STATS-design-stats.md#s-stats-combat-getter-the-attack-and-armor-getter-0xc34a50-fully-decoded)) `max(0, base × (1 + Σ))`, 0 when `base` is 0, with `base` the ship's light, heavy, sub or torpedo attack or armor, plus `submarine_attack` (flat) for a submarine's attacks. Σ: by the ship type's sub-unit `type` (not the role) `navy_submarine_attack_factor`/`navy_capital_ship_attack_factor`/`navy_screen_attack_factor` (armor: the `_defence_factor`s), so heavy cruisers take the capital terms and carriers none; for attacks `naval_night_attack × darkness`; `naval_damage_factor` (armor: `naval_defense_factor`) from the country, the admiral and the experience level; for attacks the admiral's adjusters (S-ADMIRAL-ADJUST). The armor is what the piercing tier is read against. Both damage terms enter the S-DMG hit multiplier again, so each counts twice [RE + CAL: logged in-game hits, [LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)]. While the side's pride of the fleet is afloat, the commanding admiral's `naval_has_potf_in_combat_attack`/`_defense` (S-POTF-COMBAT). The naval terrain's `units` adjuster for the ship's sub-unit (attack or defence) and, for attacks, the admiral's terrain adjuster (S-TERRAIN). Not modelled: fuel, the `_against` terms (they key on the controller of the sea province, likely never active);
  - AA (0xc3a470) `(anti_air_attack + navy_anti_air_attack) × (1 + navy_anti_air_attack_factor)`, no clamp, which the fleet AA sum, preemptive AA and air target weights read (A-AA-REDUCE, A-AA-PREEMPT, A-TARGET);
  - visibility (0xc3aa20, 0xc3ab80) `max(0, visibility × (1 + navy_visibility))`, read by the hit profile (S-HIT-SHIP) and the submarine reveal (S-SUB);
  - combat speed (0xc3a740) `speed × max(0.1, 1 + naval_speed_factor + the naval terrain's movement adjuster)`, read by the hit profile (S-HIT-SHIP) and the escape speed (S-RETREAT-SPEED); the admiral's and the weather's `naval_speed_factor` come through the modifier sum (S-TERRAIN, S-WEATHER); fuel is not modelled;
  - maximum organization (0xc3d4a3) `max(0.001, (max_organisation + navy_org) × (1 + navy_org_factor))`, the cap of organization and its regain, and the crew factor's ratio (S-HIT-MODS);
  - the retreat decision chance (S-RETREAT) and organization regain (S-ORG-REGAIN).
- **S-STATS-COUNTRY** [RE + CAL]: country modifiers. A technology's top-level keys (e.g. `smoke_generator`'s `naval_retreat_speed = 0.2`) are modifiers of the country, not of an equipment or sub-unit, and the ship modifier sum `0xc38760` gathers them for every ship of the country; the combat getters read them through it (the member modifier getter `0x1972570` adds the screening bonuses on top). Values of the same key add up over the side's researches. In the data a research lists them as `countryModifiers` by game key; they apply to every ship of the side, whatever the research's `appliesToRoles`/`appliesToTypes` (which scope only its stat blocks). The engine reads the retreat speeds of S-RETREAT-SPEED, the hit chance terms of S-HIT-MODS (`naval_hit_chance`, `naval_light_gun_hit_chance_factor`, `naval_heavy_gun_hit_chance_factor`, `naval_torpedo_hit_chance_factor`), the crit terms of S-CRIT (`naval_critical_score_chance_factor`, `critical_receive_chance`, `naval_critical_effect_factor`) and the S-STATS-COMBAT getters (`naval_speed_factor`, `navy_org`, `navy_org_factor`, `naval_morale`, `naval_morale_factor`, `naval_retreat_chance`, `naval_retreat_chance_after_initial_combat`); the loader warns about any other key and ignores it (`BattleParticipant.ModelledCountryModifiers`). Doctrines and spirits join the same sum (S-DOCTRINE-EFFECT); so do the attack, armor, AA and visibility keys above, `naval_torpedo_cooldown_factor` (S-COOLDOWN), `navy_casualty_on_hit` (S-DMG-APPLY) and the positioning, screening, torpedo and air keys of S-POS-MOD, S-SCREEN, S-TARGET-TORP, S-SUB and air.md. [RE] Some keys have **no effect in battle from a country** (`BattleParticipant.CountryModifiersWithoutBattleEffect`, shown as such in the GUI): the game reads `naval_enemy_fleet_size_ratio_penalty_factor` (Naval Tripwire, Localized Sea Power), `naval_has_potf_in_combat_attack`/`_defense` (Line of Battle) and `naval_enemy_retreat_chance` only from an admiral's own modifiers, which country modifiers never reach (`leader+0x418`, [finding](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-STATS-COUNTRY-modifier-sources.md); the admiral's own are simulated, S-ADMIRAL); `navy_casualty_on_sink` only changes the manpower returned to the country's pool; `naval_coordination` has no combat reader.

## S-DOCTRINE: doctrines [RE + PDX]

Finding: [S-DOCTRINE-doctrines.md](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-DOCTRINE-doctrines.md). The 1.19 doctrine system, read from `common/doctrines` and its `_documentation.md` files; data: `doctrines/<folder>.json`, written by `NavySimulator import-doctrines` (GameData `Import/DoctrineImporter`) with the game's IDs and names. Code: `Engine/Doctrines/` (`DoctrineRules`), `SetupLoader.ValidateDoctrines`/`BuildDoctrines`, `BattleParticipant`. Doctrine experience and mastery gain are not modelled: the scenario sets them.
- **Setup**: a side has per folder (`naval`, `air`) one grand doctrine and, for any of the tracks that grand doctrine opens, one subdoctrine whose `track` lists that track, with the track's mastery: `"doctrines": { "naval": { "grandDoctrine": "new_fleet_in_being", "tracks": { "capital_ships": { "subdoctrine": "line_of_battle", "mastery": 300 } } } }`. Unknown IDs, a grand doctrine of another folder, a track the grand doctrine does not open, a subdoctrine of another track and negative mastery are errors. A subdoctrine in two tracks (without `allow_in_multiple_tracks`) and two `xor` subdoctrines in one folder are warnings: the game would not allow them, the simulator runs them.
- **S-DOCTRINE-MASTERY** [RE] (0x147c000, 0x9e4da0, 0xfc87b0): track mastery is cumulative; rewards unlock in script order, reward *i* at `Σ_{j≤i} cost_j`, where a reward's cost is its `mastery`, or `NDoctrines.DEFAULT_REWARD_MASTERY` (100) when it is missing or 0 (five default rewards: 100, 200, …, 500). A track is complete when all its rewards are unlocked; the grand doctrine's milestone at the track's index then applies too, while the track stays complete. Mastery has no cap; in the game, gain past completion goes to a bank (`MASTERY_BANK_*`), which only matters when the subdoctrine is swapped. The game's `has_mastery_level` counts unlocked rewards.
- **S-DOCTRINE-EFFECT** [RE] (0x1529a80, 0x13cd240, 0x13cdc50): the active parts of a setup are the grand doctrine, each slotted subdoctrine, its unlocked rewards and the milestones of complete tracks. Each part's "activation effects" are, by script form:
  - numbers at top level and in a `modifier` block: **country modifiers**, added to the country's modifier sum (`country+0x5b8`) with the researches' (S-STATS-COUNTRY). The documented plural `modifiers` block has no case in the reader and is not applied (medium; no vanilla or TFB doctrine uses it);
  - a block named after a sub-unit (`battleship = { max_organisation = 5 hg_attack = 0.05 }`) or sub-unit category: a **sub-unit bonus**, added with weight 1 to the country's per-sub-unit stats after the technologies (0x101b1b0 → 0x193d2a0), so it reaches ships like a technology (S-STATS-SHIP: equipment stats × (1 + Σ), the additive set also + Σ, organization flat);
  - `equipment_bonus = { <key> = { … } }`: an **equipment bonus** (the named-bonus path 0xe60110, as `add_equipment_bonus`), multiplicative in the design's multiplier sum (S-STATS-BONUS). The key is an equipment, archetype, type keyword (`capital_ship`, `screen_ship`, `carrier`, `submarine`, `convoy`) or equipment group, matched against the design's hull (0xc97e00, S-MIO-MATCH): the modules a design installs and its ship type do not count. [ASSUMED] Every bonus applies, `instant` or not: in the game a non-instant one reaches only designs made (or copied from parents) after the doctrine;
  - `effect` runs once in country scope and is never undone; it and `enable_tactic` are not run (milestone `effect`s only count milestones in variables).
  Stats the ship model lacks (supply and fuel consumption, range, coordination, mines) and country modifiers the combat rules do not read are listed per side as not simulated (`BattleParticipant.IgnoredEffects`, the battle notes and the GUI).
- **Air folder** (2026-10-20): air doctrines are simulated like the naval folder. Their country modifiers join the side's sum (the air keys the naval strike, carrier sorties and the region's air war read, A-STATS-MODS), and their sub-unit blocks for plane sub-units and categories (`category_carrier_nav_bomber = { naval_strike_attack = 0.05 }`) raise the side's plane stats (A-STATS in air.md). Air keys with no reader in a naval battle (country `carrier_traffic`, strategic bombing, CAS, intel, costs) are not listed as ignored.
- [CAL] (2026-10-19) The mixed-fleet battle with Fleet in Being, all four tracks complete and Decisive Battle on both sides (`jukki_usa_vs_bc_japan.json`, written into the save by `tools/ingame/make_save.py`), logged with rolls, replays with all 10,951 shots matching and both sides' HP damage exact; what is left (organization regain +0.02/h, a few experience values) is what the doctrine-free replays show too. The logged modifier holders show the country modifiers the engine has, and no admiral. A first run of the same save and seed went differently in the game (Japan +0.075 positioning in hours 1–29, all hits ×0.89–0.93, from hour 30 Japan's hit chances ×0.965) and did not repeat; its cause is not known.
- **Open**: the non-instant equipment bonus timing; equipment bonuses in saves (not written by `make_save.py`); the first run's one-off game-side change.

## S-SPIRIT: navy and air spirits [PDX + RE]

Ideas of the categories `common/idea_tags` gives `type = navy_spirit` (`naval_academy_spirit`, `navy_spirit`, `naval_command_spirit`) or `air_spirit`, from `common/ideas`; data: `spirits/navy-spirits.json` and `spirits/air-spirits.json` (imported with the doctrines). A spirit's `modifier` block gives country modifiers and its `equipment_bonus` equipment bonuses, as for doctrines (S-DOCTRINE-EFFECT); `research_bonus` is not simulated. Its `available` trigger's `has_doctrine` conditions are kept as groups (a top-level or `AND` condition is a group of one, an `OR` of `has_doctrine`s one group); every group must name a doctrine the side has. `has_doctrine` is true for the active grand doctrine and every slotted subdoctrine, in any folder, whatever the track's mastery [RE] (0x52bce0). Other conditions (`has_naval_academy`, `has_dlc`, `tag`) are shown, not checked. A spirit whose doctrines are missing, and two spirits in one category, are warnings; the spirit is simulated anyway. [PDX] Each category has one slot (`common/idea_tags/00_idea.txt`, one `slot` line each; TFB does not override it), so a country holds one academy, one navy and one naval command spirit. The hand-written [LEGACY] spirits with stat blocks are gone.

## S-MIO: military industrial organizations [RE]

The MIOs of `common/military_industrial_organization/organizations` whose `equipment_type` names ship equipment, with the policies of `.../policies` and the equipment groups of `common/equipment_groups`; data: `mios/<game file>.json`, `mio-policies/naval-policies.json`, `equipment-groups/naval-groups.json`, written by `NavySimulator import-mios` (GameData `Import/MioImporter`, TFB dev checkout over vanilla: 123 MIOs, 1,794 traits, 11 policies). Finding for the include and the tree: [S-MIO-include-and-tree.md](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-MIO-include-and-tree.md). Code: `Engine/Mios/` (`MioRules`), `SetupLoader.ValidateMios`/`BuildMios`, `BattleParticipant`. A design names its MIO (`mioID`, its design team), and a scenario side can assign another MIO, or none, to any design of its fleet (`designMios`, by design ID): in the game the assignment is the country's variant's, so two sides with the same design can use different MIOs (`BattleParticipant.MioOf`). Which traits are unlocked and which policy is attached belong to the side too (scenario `mios`, by MIO ID), as in the game, where the trait tree is the country's. Funds, MIO size, research and production bonuses are not modelled; `production_bonus` and `organization_modifier` are recorded and shown as "no effect in battle".

- **S-MIO-IMPORT** [RE] (0xa54150 include, 0xa507b0 trait merge, 0xa4e2c0 positions): with `include = X`, each field of the including MIO is taken from X only when the own one is unset (missing or an empty block, so PHI Cavite's `equipment_type = { }` gets X's) and not in `delete_included_values`; lists are replaced whole, never appended. The initial trait is merged (below) unless `delete_included_values` names `initial_trait`. The tree is the own `trait`/`add_trait` blocks (one reader) in file order, then X's traits in X's order: a token in `remove_trait` is skipped (an own block with it then stays unmerged); else an `override_trait` with the token is appended with X's trait merged in; else an own block with the token gets X's trait merged in, in place (vanilla's BEL, NOR and PHI shipyards write `trait = { token = generic_mio_trait_submarine_hunters limit_to_equipment_type = { screen_ship } }` to narrow a generic trait); else it is copied. An override that matches nothing is left out (logged). The trait merge takes each key the own trait leaves unset (missing, an empty block, `special_trait_background = no`) and does not list in its own `delete_included_values`; `parent`, `any_parent` and `all_parents` are one key, so an own `any_parent` drops every included parent group (GER Deutsche Werke's splinter protection then needs only the raider doctrine); `equipment_bonus` and `production_bonus` are merged per kind (no included equipment stat when the own trait has any). Position: `position` (unset is (−1, −1), not drawn on the grid), plus the position of the first trait with the `relative_position_id` token in the same tree (recursive); an unknown relative trait gives (0, 0). Names follow the documentation's localisation rules (`TAG_key` first, the owner's tag taken from `allowed`). An MIO whose `allowed` is `always = no` is an archetype (no country has it); one with `is_debug = yes` exists only in debug mode; both are imported, marked.
- **S-MIO-MATCH** [RE] (0xa0a600, 0xc95730, 0xc949d0; finding [S-MIO-equipment-matching.md](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-MIO-equipment-matching.md), checked against the 173 MIO variants of a TFB 1936 save): the game matches the variant's **hull**, not its installed modules. A token list matches a design when a token is exactly (case-sensitive) its hull (`ship_hull_cruiser_2`; the `parent` chain is not followed), the hull's archetype (`ship_hull_cruiser`), one of the hull's type keywords, or an equipment group whose members (nested groups expanded) hold one of those (`ShipDesign.EquipmentBonusTypes`). The type keywords are the archetype's `type` plus the `add_equipment_type` of the hull's default modules, normalized: `capital_ship` drops `screen_ship`, `submarine` and `convoy`; else `submarine` drops `screen_ship` and `convoy`; else `screen_ship` drops `convoy`. So TFB's torpedo cruiser, panzerschiff and coastal defense hulls are `capital_ship` and not `screen_ship`, while a heavy cruiser on a plain cruiser hull stays `screen_ship` whatever battery it carries. Sub-unit names and unknown tokens match nothing. (The game reads the hull's own `default_modules`; the import stores them with inherited ones, the same set for every vanilla and TFB hull.) An MIO can be assigned to the designs its `equipment_type` matches (the game also allows it when the attached policy has a key that matches; otherwise it drops the assignment on load, the simulator warns and applies it anyway). A trait reaches a design when its `limit_to_equipment_type` is unwritten or matches; the MIO's `equipment_type` is not tested again, and the initial trait follows its own limit (TFB's Newport News: +5 % HP for carriers only).
- **S-MIO-COUNTRY** [PDX]: a side may name the country it plays (`country`, a tag of `countries/`, imported from `common/country_tags` without the dynamic tags). An MIO is available to a country when its `allowed` names the tag (`tag`/`original_tag`, any key case; a scripted trigger such as `NOR_AAT = yes` is looked into), or names no tag outside a `NOT` and does not name the tag inside one (TFB's generic escort yard: every country without its own escort yard); archetypes and debug-mode MIOs are available to none. Other conditions (`has_dlc`, focuses) are not checked. The GUI offers only the country's MIOs; an MIO the side uses that its country cannot have is a warning (`MioCountries.IsAvailable`).
- **S-MIO-TREE** [RE] (0x13af360 unlock check, 0xdba8e0 parents): the initial trait is always unlocked. Each `parent = { traits num_parents_needed }` (default 1), `any_parent` (needs 1) and `all_parents` (needs all listed) is a group, and every group must hold (AND): it needs that many of its traits unlocked. An empty `all_parents = { }` always holds; an empty `any_parent = { }` never does. A trait with no group can be unlocked at any time. The unlock check looks only at the candidate's own `mutually_exclusive` list, so a one-sided pair can both be unlocked (the listing trait first), a reciprocal pair (all of vanilla's) cannot. A side may unlock any set of traits: a group that does not hold and a reciprocal pair are warnings, and are simulated anyway. `visible`/`available` triggers (e.g. `has_tech = centimetric_radar`) and the MIO's trait points are not checked.
- **S-MIO-POLICY** [RE] (0xa4b260, 0xa4a7f0): one policy per MIO. Its `equipment_bonus = { <key> = { stats } same_as_mio = { stats } }`: a keyed block reaches the designs its key matches (S-MIO-MATCH), `same_as_mio` the designs the MIO's `equipment_type` matches; the blocks join the MIO's sum (S-STATS-MIO), e.g. Stable Gunnery Platforms adds 0.1 to the light and heavy gun hit chance factors. Production stats have no effect on ships. [PDX] A policy is allowed when it has no `has_mio_equipment_type` condition in `allowed`, or one names an entry of the MIO's `equipment_type` (the trigger looks the name up in the list); another policy is a warning. `available = { has_mio_size > 5 }` is not checked.
- **S-MIO-TIMING** [RE] (0xbd3b80, 0xdbc860): the game takes a variant's MIO bonus when the variant is created or its MIO changes (saved as `design_team_bonus`) and does not refresh it when a trait is unlocked; it counts unlocked traits only while the MIO's `visible` and `available` triggers hold (the initial trait and the policy always count). The simulator gives every design the side's current traits, as for designs made after the traits were unlocked, and does not check the triggers.

## S-ADMIRAL: admirals [RE + PDX]

A side may have an admiral commanding its fleet (`admiral` in the scenario side): an overall skill level, attack, defense, maneuvering and coordination levels and traits, as a character's `navy_leader` block has them. `NavySimulator import-leaders` (GUI: Settings → Import admirals) writes every character of `common/characters` with a `navy_leader` block to `admirals/` (country: the one whose `history/countries` file recruits it, at any depth; else the tag the ID starts with; else the file's tag), the traits of `type = navy` or `all` to `leader-traits/` and the `type = navy` levels of the skill files to `leader-skills/`. A side's admiral is a copy: pick one (the GUI lists the side's country's admirals) and edit it, or make one up. With one task force per side, the side's admiral is both its commanding admiral (the game takes the highest skill among the admirals of the side's task forces, 0x161e5c0) and the fleet admiral each ship's modifier sum falls back to (0xd64510), so every read below uses it. TFB's BUL and ROM history files recruit the `debug_*` admirals of `_debug_allied.txt` and `_debug_axis.txt`, and its `USA.txt` defines some admirals twice under two IDs; the import keeps them as the game has them.

- **S-ADMIRAL-SKILL** [PDX + RE] (0xc11540, 0xc24870, [finding](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-ADMIRAL-leader-combat.md#3-skill-levels)): each skill's level picks a level of `common/unit_leader/00_<skill>_skills.txt`, whose `modifier` joins the admiral's container A: attack `naval_damage_factor`, defense `naval_defense_factor`, maneuvering `positioning` and `naval_retreat_speed`, coordination `naval_coordination` (no combat reader, S-STATS-COUNTRY); the overall skill's navy levels are empty (commented out in vanilla and TFB), so it only decides the commanding admiral (highest overall skill). Vanilla adds 0.05 attack or defense per level, TFB 0.025. The level is the set level plus the traits' adds (`attack_skill = -1` of `demoted`), and the file is read at `clamp(level, 1, highest defined level)`: 0 or less reads level 1, above the highest (10 in vanilla and TFB) reads the highest; no define caps it. The overall skill is read without the clamp. `building_module_modifier` is what the admiral gives from a naval headquarters, not to the fleet it commands (0x1622d60, the building-module term of the ship modifier sum, [finding](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-STATS-COUNTRY-modifier-sources.md)): imported, not simulated.
- **S-ADMIRAL-TRAIT** [PDX]: a trait's `modifier` and `non_shared_modifier` numbers; the blocks inside `modifier` are adjusters, by naval terrain (`water_deep_ocean = { attack defence movement }`) or `night`; `sub_unit_modifiers = { destroyer = { naval_speed_factor = 0.05 units = { attack defence } } }` gives modifiers (and adjusters) to the ships of one sub-unit, the ship type's ID; `attack_skill` and the like add skill levels. `parent`, `any_parent`, `all_parents` and `mutually_exclusive` make the trait tree; a trait the game would not allow (missing parent, mutually exclusive pair) is a warning and is simulated anyway. Scripted parts (`on_add`, `enable_ability`) are not simulated.
- **S-ADMIRAL-MOD** [RE] (0xc21fc0, 0xc21570, findings [S-STATS-COUNTRY](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-STATS-COUNTRY-modifier-sources.md#the-admirals-containers), [S-ADMIRAL](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-ADMIRAL-leader-combat.md)): container B (`leader+0x298`) is the traits' `modifier` plus `non_shared_modifier`, never scaled. Container A (`leader+0x418`) is the same plus every skill level's `modifier`, scaled by the leader efficiency `max(0, size × task-force cap factor + skill_bonus_factor of B)` (0xc19fa0). A navy leader's size factors are always 1 and the cap factor is 1 up to `ADMIRAL_TASKFORCE_CAP` (vanilla 10, TFB 5) task forces, so with one task force the efficiency is `max(0, 1 + skill_bonus_factor)` (status traits: `wounded`/`sick` −0.5, `reassigned` −1). The scaling 0x60fbd0 follows each modifier's definition flags: a "positive is good" key (`naval_damage_factor`, `positioning`, hit chances, org, speed, retreat chance, …) is scaled whatever its sign; a "positive is bad" key (`navy_visibility`, `naval_critical_effect_factor`, `critical_receive_chance`, `naval_torpedo_cooldown_factor`, `naval_torpedo_reveal_chance_factor`, casualty keys; `HardcodedValues.LeaderModifiersScaledWhenNegative`) only where negative. With efficiency 1 nothing changes. An admiral moved between fleets has efficiency 0 for `UNIT_LEADER_MODIFIER_COOLDOWN_ON_GROUP_CHANGE` (15) days × (1 + `reassignment_duration_factor`); the side's admiral is taken as assigned long before the battle. No country modifier reaches either container, nor the efficiency (a country `skill_bonus_factor` has no effect). Every ship modifier read of the side (the S-STATS-COMBAT getters, S-HIT-MODS, S-CRIT, S-RETREAT, S-COOLDOWN, S-TARGET-TORP, S-SUB, carrier sorties) adds A and the traits' `sub_unit_modifiers` for the ship's sub-unit, scaled like A (0xc38760; `Ship.AdmiralModifiers`); the side reads of `positioning` and `screening_efficiency` add A (0x15c70b0, S-POS-MOD, S-SCREEN). `screening_without_screens` is read from the country alone (no admiral, no location), and the plane getters' naval strike and carrier air factors from the country alone (0xf60f10, 0x1978e30), so an admiral's values of those have no effect. Read only from an admiral: the **enemy** admiral's A `naval_enemy_fleet_size_ratio_penalty_factor` scales the own fleet-size penalty (S-POS-SIZE); the **enemy** admiral's B `naval_enemy_retreat_chance` scales each ship's retreat chance (S-RETREAT); the own admiral's A `naval_has_potf_in_combat_attack`/`_defense` applies while the side's pride of the fleet is afloat (S-POTF-COMBAT); the **attacker's** admiral's A `naval_enemy_positioning_in_initial_attack` joins the defender's positioning in the first `COMBAT_INITIAL_DURATION` hours (with the defender's country value; no vanilla or TFB script uses the key, not modelled). An admiral's `naval_critical_effect_factor` (Crisis Magician −0.5) scales the damaged parts' modifiers (S-CRIT-PART).
- **S-ADMIRAL-ADJUST** [RE] (0xc34a50, 0xc21280, [finding](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-ADMIRAL-leader-combat.md#5-terrain-and-night-adjusters)): for attacks (the target's armor is read with no admiral, 0x1970c12), the attack getter adds the commanding admiral's `units` adjuster `attack` for the ship's sub-unit and its night adjuster `attack × darkness`. The adjusters are scaled by the size efficiency only (1 for one task force), never by `skill_bonus_factor`; `movement` has no combat reader (combat speed takes only `naval_speed_factor` and the terrain's own adjuster). The trait's adjuster for the battle's naval terrain (S-TERRAIN) adds its `attack` too.
- **Not modelled**: experience, skill and trait gain during the battle; medals (`unit_medals`) and leader-bound timed entries; the naval headquarters term; `sub_unit_modifiers` of air sub-units (`cv_cas`, `cv_nav_bomber`; no ship has them); admirals of other task forces, the over-capacity factor and the reassignment cooldown; `naval_enemy_positioning_in_initial_attack` (S-POS). Keys combat does not read from an admiral are listed as not simulated (`BattleParticipant.IgnoredAdmiralEffects`); `naval_coordination` and leader bookkeeping keys (`experience_gain_factor`, `promote_cost_factor`, ...) have no battle effect.

**Code.** `Engine/Leaders/` (`Admiral`, `AdmiralRules`, `LeaderTrait`, `LeaderSkills`); `BattleParticipant` (sets `Ship.Admiral` and `Ship.AdmiralModifiers`); `MemberModifierRules.ShipModifier`, `CombatStatRules.CombatValue`, `BattleSession.SideModifier`, `PositioningRules.FleetSizePenalty`, `RetreatRules.RetreatDecisionChance`. Data: `GameData/Import/LeaderImporter`, `SetupLoader` (validation: unknown traits and negative levels are errors; levels above the files' and trait-tree problems are warnings).

## S-POTF: pride of the fleet [RE]

A side may name one design of its fleet as its pride of the fleet (`prideOfTheFleet` in the scenario side); the first ship of that design in the fleet is the country's pride of the fleet (PotF, `country+0x250`; ships of one design start alike). [Finding](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-POTF-pride-of-the-fleet.md).

- **S-POTF-SHIP** [RE] (0xc387e2): the PotF carries the static modifier block `pride_of_the_fleet` (`common/modifiers/00_static_modifiers.txt`; vanilla and TFB `critical_receive_chance −0.5`, `experience_gain_navy_unit_factor 0.25`) in every read of its ship modifier sum, in every state (waiting, fighting, retreating, escaped). So gun, depth-charge and torpedo crits against it are halved (S-CRIT: `1 + score + receive`; TFB with both damage control techs `1 − 0.2 − 0.5 = 0.3`), air crits against it are 1.5 times as likely (A-CRIT: the game applies the same value as `max(0, 1 − receive)`), and it gains experience 25 % faster (S-XP-GAIN). Any other key a mod puts in the block reaches it the same way.
- **S-POTF-COMBAT** [RE] (0xc351ae): while the PotF is afloat, every ship of its side (the PotF included) adds the side's commanding admiral's container A `naval_has_potf_in_combat_attack` to its attack Σ (light, heavy, torpedo, depth charges) and `naval_has_potf_in_combat_defense` to its armor Σ, the target's armor in the piercing check included (S-STATS-COMBAT); they count once (not in the hit multiplier). The test is that the PotF's task force is in the combat, which holds in every member state until the battle ends: escaped included. It ends inside the shot that sinks the PotF (the ship is deleted and the country's handle cleared). No admiral, no term; country sources (Line of Battle) never count (S-STATS-COUNTRY). Vanilla and TFB source: the admiral trait Media Personality (0.1 / 0.1), scaled by the leader efficiency (S-ADMIRAL-MOD).
- **S-POTF-ELIGIBLE** [RE] (0xc33a50): the game lets a country assign a ship whose sub-unit `type` has `capital_ship`, or a carrier (sub-unit or equipment type `carrier`; `missile_launcher` too): vanilla battleship, battlecruiser, heavy cruiser, carrier; TFB adds the super heavy battleship and carrier. The GUI offers those designs; another design is a warning and simulated anyway, as the game does for one a history file sets (0xc34280). One per country.
- **Nothing else** reads it in battle [RE]: no target weight, target pick or air target term (enemies do not focus it), no positioning, screening, retreat or organization effect, and its sinking changes nothing for the rest of the battle. Outside the battle (not simulated): `pride_of_the_fleet_country` (war support +5 %) while assigned, `pride_of_the_fleet_sunk_temporary` (war support −10 % for `PRIDE_OF_THE_FLEET_LOST_TEMP_MODIFIER_DURATION` days) and the on_action `on_pride_of_the_fleet_sunk`. The battle events mark its sinking.
- **S-POTF-SAVE** [RE]: the country block's `pride_of_the_fleet={ id=<ship id> type=51 }`, before `pride_of_the_fleet_date_lost`, written only when set. `tools/ingame/make_save.py` writes it for each side (and removes the template's when the side has none).

**Code.** `PrideOfTheFleetRules`, `MemberModifierRules.ShipModifier`, `CombatStatRules.CombatValue`, `ExperienceRules.ModifiedGain`, `AirRules.CriticalChance`; `BattleParticipant.PrideOfTheFleet`, `Ship.PrideOfTheFleet`; data `SetupLoader` (a design outside the fleet is an error).

## S-HOUR: hour structure [RE]

Each hour:
1. Positioning (S-POS), screening (S-SCREEN) and submarine detection (S-SUB) for both sides, from the same pre-fire state. They are not recomputed after losses during the hour. [RE + LOG] The sides are updated in turn: the attacker's positioning and screening, then the defender's, so the defender's positioning sees the attacker's new screening (through the carrier sorties of the carrier ratio); a new battle's side starts unscreened. In logged TFB carrier battles the carrier-ratio helper's sums (0x161eeb0) showed this order every hour.
2. **Attacker's turn**, then **defender's turn** (S-ORDER). In a turn, every ship of the side, in fleet order:
   1. steps its submarine reveal timer (S-SUB);
   2. **Waiting**: activates if its line is active (S-ACTIVATE). Ships become *fighting*; convoys go straight to *retreating*. A ship does not fire in the hour it activates;
   3. **Fighting**: escape progress is reset; from tick `COMBAT_MIN_DURATION` (8) it may decide to retreat (S-RETREAT). A ship that starts retreating does not fire this hour. Otherwise it fires (S-COOLDOWN, S-TARGET);
   4. **Retreating**: fires, then advances its escape (S-RETREAT-SPEED).
   
   After its ships, the side's air step runs in air hours (A-*, [air.md](air.md)). If the enemy has only hidden submarines left (nothing the side can target) and no air acted, all of the side's ships start retreating.
3. Experience gain (S-XP), organization regain (S-ORG-REGAIN), end check (S-END).

The experience level the combat modifiers use is taken at the start of the hour (S-XP). A battle the game creates during play (two hostile task forces meeting) runs one update with hour counter 0 that rolls and fires nothing, so its battle hour 0 is the game hour after it. [LOG] That update still runs the side update (positioning, screening, fleet AA) and the carriers' sortie experience, so battle hour 0 starts from its screening (carrier sorties, and through them the carrier-ratio positioning, read it); the engine runs the same creation update before hour 0 (`BattleSession.CreationUpdate`); a scenario that reproduces such a battle sets `rules.startHourOfDay` to that hour (the save's hour + 1) [CAL: the daily manpower refill of a replayed battle, [LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)].

- **S-ORG-REGAIN** [RE + CAL] (0xc395a0): after the hour, every ship that is not fighting (game state ≠ 2: waiting, retreating, escaped) regains `(NMilitary.SHIP_MORALE_TO_ORG_REGAIN_BASE (0.2) + naval_morale) × max(0, 1 + naval_morale_factor + dominance)` organization, up to its maximum organization (S-STATS-COMBAT). A fire (S-CRIT-PART, `naval_morale_factor −0.8`) cuts it to 0.04. `dominance` is `NNavy.NAVAL_DOMINANCE_ORG_RECOVERY` (0.1) for a side that controls the battle's region (S-DOMINANCE), else 0: 0.22 an hour in the logged battles, the USA side until Japan takes control at a day change. The engine gives it with `rules.navalDominanceEnabled` to the side that controls the region, decided at the start and again every midnight (S-DOMINANCE-BATTLE, S-DOMINANCE-DAILY). A ship value at `+0x120` in the same factor is not identified. Retreating ships still fire, and organization enters their hit chance through the crew factor (S-HIT).

**Code.** `BattleSession.cs` (one hour per `StepHour`; `BattleEngine` runs a session to its end). Fire-exchange groups: with `COMBAT_MAX_GROUPS` (1) each side forms one group paired with the other side's, so groups have no effect in vanilla and are not modelled.

## S-DOMINANCE: naval dominance [RE + CAL; the region over time ASSUMED]

The game keeps naval dominance per strategic sea region and country, built by task forces on missions there. In combat it only changes organization regain (S-ORG-REGAIN). [Finding](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-DOMINANCE-naval-dominance.md).

- **S-DOMINANCE-SHIP** [RE + CAL] (0xbd5d60, for a ship through its variant, 0xc33240): `naval_dominance_factor × (1 + (speed − S) / 2S + (range − R) / 2R + heavy attack × H + carrier size × C)`, with S `NNavy.DOMINANCE_PER_SHIP_PER_SPEED_NEUTRAL` (20), R `..._RANGE_NEUTRAL` (2000), H `..._HEAVY_GUN_ATTACK` (0.01) and C `..._CARRIER_SIZE` (0.1; TFB 0.2). A term whose neutral value is 0 is 0; nothing is clamped. The stats are the equipment variant's: the design with the side's equipment bonuses (S-STATS-DESIGN, S-STATS-BONUS), without technology and doctrine sub-unit bonuses or damage. `naval_dominance_factor` belongs to the hull equipment (TFB: light hull 2 30, cruiser hull 2 115, heavy hull 3 375, super heavy 800), imported as the hull's `navalDominanceFactor`; ranges come from `naval_range` (`navalRange`). [CAL] The CL3 battle's save holds the Black Sea's dominance of its two task forces after their patrol, BUL 17376.9375 and ROM 17238: the sums of this value over the two fleets, to the decimal (a unit test).
- **S-DOMINANCE-REGION** [RE] (0xfafda0, 0x1003f50, 0x143c720): a task force on a mission in the region gives its country `Σ ship dominance × mission efficiency × NNavy.MISSION_DOMINANCE_RATIOS[mission]` (vanilla patrol 1.0, TFB 1.5). [CAL] Both CL3 task forces were on patrol (save `mission=1`), yet the saved values equal the plain sums, without TFB's 1.5: where the ratio and efficiency enter is not settled. With both sides on the same mission it does not change who has more. A country controls the region when its and its allies' current dominance is at least the enemies' (on a tie both do) and at least `t / (1 − t)` times the region's own value, t = `DOMINANCE_CONTROLLED_THRESHOLD_RATIO` (0.6). The Black Sea's own value is about 250 (the save's `individual_ratio` is `current / (current + own value)`), so 375 is enough there.
- **S-DOMINANCE-DAILY** [RE + LOG] (0x1003c50, [finding](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-DOMINANCE-naval-dominance.md#s-dominance-daily-the-daily-update-0x1003c50-re--cal)): once a day, in the tick where the day changes (00:00), after its combat hour and before its organization regain, each country's target is the dominance of the ships in its task forces on the region (sunk ships leave; escaped and retreating ones stay; HP does not count). With a target the current value gains `trunc(target × DOMINANCE_DAILY_GAIN_FACTOR)` (0.02; TFB 0.03), capped at the target, so sunk ships take it straight down to the new target, and the target becomes `decline_from`; a country with no task force left loses `trunc(decline_from × DOMINANCE_DAILY_LOSS_FACTOR)` (0.04) a day, down to 0. Control is then decided from the current values until the next day change. A side starts at `navalDominanceStart` (the save's region `current`), else its ships' sum, which `make_save.py` writes unless the scenario sets a value; the `jukki_usa_vs_bc_japan_battle_start` save kept the CL3 template's values, ROM 17238 and BUL 17376.9375, which its scenario `jukki_usa_vs_bc_japan_no_doctrines.json` sets. [LOG] The decoded rule gives every hand-over hour of 127 logged battles (the earlier calibrated model, a gradual loss at 02:00, got 101); with it the 11 roll-logged baseline replays and the 10 MIO + admiral replays have no organization difference before their last hour, and every constant-roll battle matches every shot (S-REPLAY-CONSTANT). Not modelled: the strike-force term, the buildings multiplier (1 in region 30) and the region's own value in the control minimum (about 250 in the Black Sea: never binding here). Intel (`NIntel.NAVAL_DOMINANCE_INTEL_*`) can lower a country's dominance; it did not in these saves.
- **S-DOMINANCE-BATTLE** [ASSUMED] (`BattleRules.NavalDominanceEnabled`, scenario `rules.navalDominanceEnabled`, default off): each side's target is S-DOMINANCE-SHIP summed over its ships, as if both patrolled the region; its current value starts at `navalDominanceStart` or the target and follows S-DOMINANCE-DAILY; the side with more current dominance (both on a tie, neither with 0) controls the region. Other task forces, the region's own value and intel are left out. `tools/ingame/make_save.py` writes these sums into the battle region of a test save, so in-game tests start the same way. With it on, the 11 replays of `jukki_usa_vs_bc_japan` have 3,825 end-of-hour organization differences instead of 10,210: none before the game hands control to Japan, then 0.02 an hour, plus the battle's last hour (S-REPLAY).

**Code.** `NavalDominanceRules` (`ShipDominance`, `SideDominance`, `DailyUpdate`, `IsDominant`), `BattleSession.SetNavalDominance` / `UpdateNavalDominance` (the hourly log notes each change of control), `ExperienceRules.HourlyOrganizationRegain`. The battle notes give both sums and who has dominance; `NavySimulator list-ships` gives each design's `navalDominance`.

## S-ORDER: firing order [RE]

The attacker's whole side fires before the defender's, every hour. Damage applies immediately per shot, so a defender ship sunk by the attacker never fires that hour. Ships are processed in fleet (list) order, not shuffled. This gives the attacker a real first-strike edge; `BattleEngineTests.Simulate_MirrorMatchFavoursTheAttacker` measures it.

The previous `Interleaved` and `Simultaneous` variants are removed.

## S-POS: positioning [RE]

`positioning = clamp(BASE_POSITIONING - sizePenalty + detectionBonus, 0, 1)`, summed and clamped once.
- **S-POS-SIZE**: every ship in the battle counts 1, all lines and retreating ships included. If `own >= MIN_SHIPS_FOR_HIGHER_SHIP_RATIO_PENALTY` (4): `sizePenalty = clamp((own/enemy - 1) × HIGHER_SHIP_RATIO_POSITIONING_PENALTY_FACTOR (0.25), 0, MAX_POSITIONING_PENALTY_FROM_HIGHER_SHIP_RATIO (0.75))`. [RE] The game multiplies the penalty by `1 + naval_enemy_fleet_size_ratio_penalty_factor` of the **enemy's commanding admiral** only (container A, S-ADMIRAL-MOD; before the cap); the country modifier (Naval Tripwire) never reaches it.
- **S-POS-DETECT**: `bonus = (ΣownSurfaceDetection - ΣenemySurfaceDetection) / 100 × RELATIVE_SURFACE_DETECTION_TO_POSITIONING_FACTOR (0.01)`, at most `MAX_POSITIONING_BONUS_FROM_SURFACE_DETECTION` (0.25), applied only if positive. Ten ships at 40 against ten at 20 gives only +0.02. A ship's surface detection is the effective one (0xc3aae0, `CombatStatRules.SurfaceDetection`): `max(0, stat × (1 + naval_detection))` through the ship modifier sum, so the region's weather lowers it by the ship's weather factor (TFB light rain −0.1; super-heavies none) [RE + CAL: the constant-roll mixed-fleet battle with rain logged Japan's positioning 0.78481 where bare detection gives 0.79372, and its fleet AA by the same factor; with the term every shot replays]. The carrier part (`carrier_surface_detection` × deck fill) is not added. Damaged parts lower it (S-STATS-PARTS): a short-circuited radar takes its ship's detection to 0 [CAL].
- **S-POS-DMG**: `DAMAGE_PENALTY_ON_MINIMUM_POSITIONING (0.5) × (1 - positioning)` is subtracted inside the additive damage multiplier (S-DMG).

[CAL] In 12 logged TFB battles (`jukki_usa_vs_bc_japan`) positioning matched the formula in all 2,240 side-hours, counting the ships at the end of the previous hour as above, with TFB's values (size factor 0.4, at most 1.2, only from 60 own ships; detection bonus at most 0.1), and every gun hit fitted the damage multiplier with TFB's penalty 0.8 at positioning 0.65 to 1 ([LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)).
- **S-POS-SCREEN**: see S-SCREEN.

**Carrier ratio** [RE] (0x161c217): `penalty = clamp((1 - ownA / enemyA) × HIGHER_CARRIER_RATIO_POSITIONING_PENALTY_FACTOR × enemyB, 0, MAX_CARRIER_RATIO_POSITIONING_PENALTY_FACTOR)`, where `A` = Σ sortie efficiency × planes over a side's carriers and `B` = their mean sortie efficiency × min(1, planes / CARRIER_PLANES_AMOUNT_FOR_POSITIONING). No penalty if the enemy has no carrier air. [RE + LOG] The sums run over the side's air objects (one per wing pool): a sunk carrier's stay with sortie 0 (they count in B's mean), an escaped carrier's leave; the planes are the air objects' counted planes (`airfx+0x5c`, refreshed only at air steps and by strike losses, A-STACK). With these the engine's positioning matched the logged side values in all but the first hour of each replayed carrier battle.

- **S-POS-MOD** [RE, name medium]: the side's `positioning` modifier (country: doctrines and spirits, e.g. Efficient Communications +0.1; the admiral's container A: maneuvering skill, S-ADMIRAL) is added to the sum before the clamp (`0x161bb4c`).

**Not modelled** (no inputs for them yet): the defender's initial-phase modifier during `COMBAT_INITIAL_DURATION` (`naval_enemy_positioning_in_initial_attack`: the defender's country plus the attacker's admiral, [RE]; no script uses it); the penalty for ships joining a running battle (`POSITIONING_PENALTY_FOR_SHIPS_JOINED_COMBAT_AFTER_IT_STARTS` with hourly decay); the convoy-escort dominance bonus. Carriers' detection should include `carrier_surface_detection` scaled by how full the deck is (needs air).

## S-SCREEN: screening efficiency [RE]

- `factor = max(0.01, 1 + screening_efficiency - SCREENING_EFFICIENCY_PENALTY_ON_MINIMUM_POSITIONING (0.5) × (1 - positioning))`, with the side's `screening_efficiency`: country (Fleet Defense +0.1, doctrines) plus the admiral's container A (Fleet Protector +0.2, S-ADMIRAL).
- `screening = clamp(screens / ceil(3 × (capitals + carriers) + 0.5 × convoys) × factor, 0, 1)`; 1 if nothing needs screening.
- `carrierScreening = clamp(capitals / ceil(1 × carriers + 0.25 × convoys) × factor, 0, 1)`; 1 if nothing needs it.
- The required counts are **rounded up** (one capital plus one convoy needs 4 screens). Ships in every in-battle state count; submarines do not.
- Effects come from the static modifiers `screening_bonus` (`naval_hit_chance 0.4`, `naval_retreat_speed 0.2`, `convoy_retreat_speed 0.2`) scaled by screening, for battle-line and carrier-line ships, and `capital_screening_bonus` (`naval_retreat_speed 0.2`, `sortie_efficiency 0.1`) scaled by `1 - (1 - screening)(1 - carrierScreening)` for carrier-line ships. They are loaded from `common/modifiers` (`GameDefines.StaticModifiers`). Every key of the two blocks applies, because the combat rules read each modifier through the member modifier getter `0x1972570` (`MemberModifierRules`). TFB's `screening_bonus` has `naval_hit_chance 0.3`, `naval_heavy_gun_hit_chance_factor 1`, `naval_light_gun_hit_chance_factor 0.3` and `naval_retreat_speed 0.25`; [CAL] in the `jukki_usa_vs_bc_japan` game run the capitals' heavy and light hit chances fit `1 + (0.3 + 1) × screening + 0.15` and `1 + (0.3 + 0.3) × screening + 0.15` exactly for both sides (screening 0.64 and 0.97), while screens matched without them.

- A side with no screens whose capitals, carriers or convoys need screening gets screening `max(0, screening_without_screens)` (TFB's Armored Raiders +0.05), added after the clamp (`0x161ec40`); it is read from the country alone, not from the admiral (0x161d2d6).

**Code.** `ScreeningRules.cs`. Modifier names 0x1b1/0x1b3 are medium confidence.

## S-ACTIVATE: when lines start firing [RE]

Thresholds come from `CARRIER_ONLY_COMBAT_ACTIVATE_TIME` (0), `CAPITAL_ONLY_COMBAT_ACTIVATE_TIME` (6) and `ALL_SHIPS_ACTIVATE_TIME` (8), and depend on what is in the battle. Composition is pooled over **both sides**, counting only visible ships still in the battle; carrier-like = carriers, capital-like = capitals and submarines.
- With carriers present: carriers 0, capitals and submarines 6, screens and convoys 8.
- No carriers: capitals and submarines 0, screens and convoys `8 + 0 - 6` = 2.
- No carriers and no capital-like ships: everything 0.

A waiting ship activates at the first tick **strictly above** its threshold (always once the tick passes `ALL_SHIPS_ACTIVATE_TIME`) and fires from the next hour. So in a battle with carriers, carriers fire from hour 1, capitals from hour 7 and screens from hour 9; in a screens-only battle, from hour 1.

**Code.** `WeaponCadenceRules.ActivationThreshold`, `IsActivated`. Support ships (counted with carriers in the game) do not exist in the data model yet.

## S-COOLDOWN: weapon cadence [RE]

Three slots per ship, processed in this order: **heavy, torpedo, light**. Depth charges share the light slot. `cooldown = max(BASE_GUN_COOLDOWNS[slot] ({1, 4, 1}), MIN_GUN_COOLDOWN)`; a ship's torpedo cooldown is first `× (1 + naval_torpedo_cooldown_factor)` (0x1972a70, ship modifier sum).

Each hour, for a slot with attack: `charge = min(charge + 1, ceil(cooldown))`. If `charge >= cooldown`, **one target** is picked for the slot, then the slot fires `while charge >= cooldown: charge -= cooldown` at that target, stopping if it sinks. Guns fire every hour; torpedoes on the fourth processed hour and every fourth after; fractional cooldowns carry over; a cooldown below 1 gives several shots in one hour. Charge is per ship and slot, starts at 0 and is not reset by retreating.

**Code.** `WeaponCadenceRules.Charge`, `TrySpendShot`; `BattleSession.FireSlots`/`FireAt`.

## S-TARGET: target selection [RE]

Lines: 1 screens, 2 capitals, 3 carriers and convoys. Submarines are not on a line. Only visible ships in the battle (waiting, fighting or retreating) are candidates; hidden submarines never are. Reach decisions use the enemy's visible line counts **taken at the start of the side's turn**, so if the only reachable line is wiped out during the turn, later shots find no target and are not fired.

- **S-TARGET-LIGHT**: lines up to the closest non-empty one (`screens ? 1 : capitals ? 2 : 3`). Any shooter, carriers included.
- **S-TARGET-HEAVY**: up to line 2 when both screens and capitals are present, otherwise up to line 3.
- **S-TARGET-TORP**: **one roll** `r`: up to line 1 if `r <= screening`, else line 2 if `r <= carrierScreening`, else line 3. The shooter's `naval_torpedo_screen_penetration_factor` (member getter; Close Combat +0.05) first turns each value into `max(0, 1 - (1 - value) × (1 + penetration))`. Screens are always candidates when present. There is no fallback: a torpedo with nothing in reach fires at nothing.
- **S-TARGET-SUB**: submarines have a nearest line from their task force aggressiveness (S-RETREAT). Do Not Engage and Engage At Low Risk: line 3, or 2 if the carrier line is empty, or 1 if capitals are also gone. Engage At Medium Risk: line 2, or 1 if capitals and the carrier line are empty. Engage At High Risk and Always Engage: line 1. That combines with the torpedo roll, so a low-aggressiveness submarine facing a fully screened convoy fires at nothing. Submarines also use their light slot under the same rule.
- **S-TARGET-DC**: the light slot may target revealed submarines as well as its surface lines; the submarine's weight needs a shooter stat above zero. That stat is not identified; sub detection is [ASSUMED]. A ship whose only weapon is depth charges never picks a target.
- **S-TARGET-WEIGHT** (`HardcodedValues`):
  - Ships: base capital 2, screen 3, submarine 4, other (carrier) 1; ×15 for heavy and torpedo against capitals and carriers; ×2 for the light slot against screens. Then `× (2 - hpRatio)`.
  - Convoys: base 4, 40 from submarines; ×15 for heavy and torpedo. Then `× (1 + 10 × (1 - hpRatio))`.
  - Then an exclusive state multiplier: fighting ×1.5, retreating ×0.5, waiting ×1. Active convoys are always retreating.
  - In the game's fixed point (fx5): `hpRatio` is the fx5 quotient rounded half up, each product is truncated [RE + CAL].
  - The resulting table is the wiki's (30/2, 3/6, 15/1, 60/4, 600/40, 4).
- **Pick**: `T = trunc(U × Σweights)` in fx5; the heaviest candidate takes `[0, maxWeight]`, then the rest accumulate in list order. That is weight-proportional. [CAL] Of 120,090 picks in 11 replayed battles, double arithmetic flips one at a boundary and a truncated `hpRatio` another; the rounded ratio with truncated products fits all (`FixedPoint`). [CAL] Replayed shot by shot on 12 logged TFB battles (`jukki_usa_vs_bc_japan`, about 127,000 shots), the weights predict every shooter → target split and every HP and state bin within noise ([LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)).
- **Retreating shooters** use exactly the same rules (the wiki's "highest weight among active targets" rule is not in the game).

**Code.** `TargetingRules.cs`.

## S-HIT: hit chance [RE]

```
hit = base × profileFactor × crewFactor × modifierSum, then at least COMBAT_MIN_HIT_CHANCE (0.005)
shot hits when U < hit   (no upper cap: hit >= 1 always hits)
```
- **S-HIT-BASE**: `COMBAT_BASE_HIT_CHANCE` (0.1); against a submarine (light slot, depth charges) `× DEPTH_CHARGES_HIT_CHANCE_MULT` (1.1).
- **S-HIT-SHIP**: `100 × visibility / (max(combat speed × HIT_PROFILE_SPEED_FACTOR (0.5), 0.1) + HIT_PROFILE_SPEED_BASE (20))`; submarines use sub visibility; convoys `CONVOY_HIT_PROFILE` (85). The wiki's 1936 light cruiser (visibility 15, 32 kn → 41.7) is a unit test. The visibility is the getter's, `× (1 + navy_visibility)` (S-STATS-COMBAT; the multiplier the old spec could not place).
- **S-HIT-WEAPON**: `GUN_HIT_PROFILES[slot]` {heavy 70, torpedo 100, light 45}; depth charges `DEPTH_CHARGES_HIT_PROFILE` (100).
- `profileFactor = clamp((shipProfile / weaponProfile)², MIN_HIT_PROFILE_MULT (0), 1)`.
- `crewFactor = max(0, 1 + COMBAT_LOW_ORG_HIT_CHANCE_PENALTY (-0.5) × missingOrg + COMBAT_LOW_MANPOWER_HIT_CHANCE_PENALTY (-0.25) × missingManpower)`, both the shooter's, **added** into one factor.
- **S-HIT-MODS**: `modifierSum = 1 + naval_hit_chance + the shooter's hit chance factor for the slot` (depth charges use the light factor), one **unclamped sum**. The slot's factor is the design stat plus the modifier `naval_light_gun|heavy_gun|torpedo_hit_chance_factor`. Both modifiers are read through the member modifier getter, so each is the sum of:
  - the country modifiers (S-STATS-COUNTRY);
  - the admiral's container A and sub-unit modifiers (S-ADMIRAL-MOD);
  - `screening_bonus` × screening (vanilla `naval_hit_chance` 0.4 at full screening) for battle-line and carrier-line shooters, and `capital_screening_bonus` × combined carrier screening for the carrier line; their per-weapon entries add to the slot's factor ([CAL] with TFB's block, see S-SCREEN);
  - `night` × darkness (−0.25 at full dark), see S-NIGHT.

  Doctrines and spirits add through the country modifiers (S-DOCTRINE-EFFECT, e.g. Decisive Battle `naval_hit_chance` +0.1); the admiral's skills and traits add through the admiral's containers (S-ADMIRAL, e.g. Superior Tactician `naval_hit_chance` +0.03); the region's weather adds to the same sum, scaled by the shooter's weather factor (S-WEATHER), and the naval terrain's keys too (S-TERRAIN).
- `COMBAT_EVASION_TO_HIT_CHANCE` has no reader in 1.19.3 (dead define).

**Code.** `HitChanceRules.cs`; the modifier getter is `MemberModifierRules.Get`.

## S-REGION: where and when a battle is fought [PDX]

A scenario places the battle with `regionID` (a strategic region of `sea-regions/`), `provinceID` (one of its sea provinces; default the first; a province alone finds its region) and `startDate` (the day of battle hour 0, as the game writes dates, `1936.5.6`; the hour is `rules.startHourOfDay`). The game's calendar has 365-day years (`GameDate`). `NavySimulator import-regions` (GameData `Import/RegionImporter`, Settings → Import sea regions) writes every strategic region with sea provinces (`map/definition.csv` type `sea`) to `sea-regions/sea-regions.json`: its name, `naval_terrain`, the provinces with their map positions (the game's: the truncated mean pixel of each province in `map/provinces.bmp`, 0x140a3c0, [A-COVERAGE](../reverse-engineering/findings/A-COVERAGE-land-mission-effectiveness.md); `map/unitstacks.txt` only when the bitmap cannot be read; the two differ by up to 35 px for 360 of TFB's 3,133 sea provinces, none in region 30) and the box of the region's provinces, the size of `map/provinces.bmp`, the weather periods and the number of region weather states (highest region id + 1, land included; TFB 315), and the naval terrains of `common/terrain` to `naval-terrains/naval-terrains.json`. TFB's dev checkout over vanilla: 106 sea regions, 4 naval terrains. Code: `Engine/Environment/` (`BattleLocation`, `SeaRegion`, `BattleEnvironment`), `GameData/Loading/LocationLoader`.

Each hour the battle reads its location (0x15c5820, `BattleSession.EnvironmentAt`): the province's darkness (S-NIGHT), the region's weather and its modifier holder (S-WEATHER) and the naval terrain (S-TERRAIN). The combat rules take this `BattleEnvironment` where they took the darkness.

## S-NIGHT: night [RE + LOG]

Findings: [S-NIGHT-darkness.md](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-NIGHT-darkness.md), [S-WEATHER-weather-terms.md](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-WEATHER-weather-terms.md#4-the-province-holder-0xf197d0-is-night--darkness). Code: `DarknessRules`, `BattleSession.Darkness`.

The weather manager keeps every province's darkness and recomputes it hourly (0xf20c50): with the province's map position `(x, y)` (y from the bottom row) and the map size `W × H`,
```
tz   = (x − GMT_OFFSET) / W + 1;  lat = y / H × (NORTH_POLE_OFFSET − SOUTH_POLE_OFFSET) + SOUTH_POLE_OFFSET
c    = −cos(lat × π);  r = 1 − |c|
v[h] = normalize(r sin a, c, r cos a),  a = 6.28318 × frac(tz + h / 24)         (0xf14fe0, once per province)
θ    = −5° at 22 June (day 172) to +5° at 22 December (day 355), linear both ways   (0xf15eb0, per day)
dark = clamp((n · v[hour] + F / 2) / F, 0, 1),  n = (0, sin θ, cos θ),  F = DAY_NIGHT_FEATHER
```
`GMT_OFFSET` (2793), `SOUTH_POLE_OFFSET` (0.17), `NORTH_POLE_OFFSET` (0.93) and `DAY_NIGHT_FEATHER` (0.024) are NGraphics defines (`00_graphics.lua`, `NGraphicsDefines`); the solstice days and the ±5° are `HardcodedValues`. The engine follows the game's fx5 arithmetic (Taylor sine, integer square root; `Stats/GameMath`): a double version misses twilight values by up to 0.01. Darkness is 1 at night, 0 by day, with a ramp in at most one hour at dusk or dawn.

[LOG] A battle hour reads the darkness the weather manager computed one game hour before (it updates after the combat): battle hour h at the clock `startDate + startHourOfDay + h` sees the darkness of the hour before. In all 10 logged TFB weather battles (Black Sea from 6 May 1936, 2,934 hours) this reproduces every logged darkness to the five decimals, for one province of region 30 per run (the battle province varied within the region: 7906, 8256 or the eastern 8282/8308/8334).

The static modifier `night` is applied "multiplied by amount of darkness" through the province's holder (0xf197d0), with no weather factor: `naval_hit_chance -0.25`, `naval_retreat_speed +0.1` (and `carrier_traffic -1.0` for air), vanilla values; TFB: `carrier_traffic -0.75`, `naval_torpedo_hit_chance_factor -0.15`. The per-weapon entries add to the slot's hit factor in S-HIT-MODS. Without a province on a known map and a start date, the engine falls back to a window: darkness 1 from `rules.nightStartHour` to `rules.nightEndHour` (defaults 20 and 6, `HardcodedValues`) [ASSUMED]. A replay takes each hour's darkness from the log. `BattleRules.NightEnabled` turns night off.

[RE + CAL] **No night with weather off.** The weather manager's hourly update returns before the darkness pass while weather is disabled (0xf1c6e0), so after `weather` / `disable_weather` darkness stays 0: in-game logs of the CL3 battle read darkness 0 every hour ([LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)). The engine has no night when `BattleRules.WeatherEnabled` is false.

## S-WEATHER: weather [RE + LOG]

Findings: [S-WEATHER-weather-toggle.md](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-WEATHER-weather-toggle.md), [S-WEATHER-weather-terms.md](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-WEATHER-weather-terms.md). Code: `RegionWeather`, `MemberModifierRules.WeatherFactor`, `BattleSession.WeatherAt`.

A region's weather is one phenomenon (light rain, heavy rain, snow, blizzard from one roll; sandstorm only without them, and only over land) and, independently, arctic water. The region's modifier holder is the static block of each (`weather_rain_light`, `weather_rain_heavy`, `weather_snow`, `weather_blizzard`, `weather_sandstorm`, `weather_arctic_water`), added **whole** (0xf220d0), read from the loaded game/mod folder (`StaticModifiers`; TFB's are harsher, e.g. blizzard `naval_hit_chance -0.5`). The region's scripted `static_modifiers` (e.g. `water_modifier_shark_infested`) join the same holder in the game; not modelled. The battle's weather type (`combat+0xa8`, the log's `weather` column) is 2 light rain, 3 heavy rain, 4 snow, 5 blizzard, 0 otherwise.

Who reads it:
- **Every ship modifier read** (0xc389cb): `block[key] × (naval_weather_penalty_factor − 1 + clamp(1 + navy_weather_penalty, 0, 1))`. The hull archetypes set the stat to 1 ("this value gets -1 before its used"), so a normal ship takes the weather whole; ice-strengthened carrier decks, MIO policies and doctrines lower the stat; a country's `navy_weather_penalty` (the vanilla spirit −0.4) lowers the clamp term. A hull without the key takes none: TFB's super-heavy battleships ignore weather. So weather reaches hit chance (`naval_hit_chance`, the per-weapon factors), combat speed (`naval_speed_factor`, and through it the target's hit profile and the escape speed), retreat speed, surface detection (`naval_detection`, S-POS-DETECT) and the attack/armor getter. The member sub detection (0xc3a950) also reads `naval_detection` and `navy_submarine_detection_factor`; it is not decoded and the engine's S-SUB detection reads neither (open).
- **Side reads** (0x15c70b0): `positioning`, `screening_efficiency`, `screening_without_screens` take the block whole (S-POS-MOD, S-SCREEN; TFB heavy rain −0.1 positioning).
- **Air**, whole: carrier sorties × `clamp(1 + carrier_traffic, 0, 1)` beside the night factor (A-SORTIE; heavy rain −0.8, blizzard −1), and the naval strike targeting multiplier `1 + naval_strike` (A-HIT; light rain −0.2).

[LOG + CAL] In the 10 TFB battles of `air_cv_mirror_weather` the weather was light rain for the first 184 hours (whatever the dice seed) and the logged ship sums held TFB's rain block whole. Rain's `naval_speed_factor −0.05` slows the target, which TFB's speed-dominated hit profile turns into +9 % profile², so the game's hit chances were +4.2 % (day) and +2.9 % (night) above a weather-free engine despite the rain's −0.05 hit chance; and its `naval_strike −0.2` cut the air damage by 20 %. With the logged weather the replay matches 1,693 of 1,694 shots and every air strike of seed 1 (before: 138 hit chance and 20 air damage differences).

A scenario's `weather` is `clear` (or empty), one phenomenon for the whole battle (`rain_light`, `rain_heavy`, `snow`, `blizzard`, `sandstorm`; `arcticWater: true` adds arctic water), or `simulated` (S-WEATHER-SIM). A replay takes each hour's weather type from the log (arctic water and sandstorm log as 0 and replay as clear).

The console commands `weather` (toggle) and `disable_weather` reset the weather, rebuild every region's state and stop the hourly weather update, so the battle reads no weather [RE, medium: the rebuilt state is not decoded]. `BattleRules.WeatherEnabled = false` (scenario `rules.weatherEnabled`) is that setting: the scenario's weather is ignored and there is no night (S-NIGHT). Use it for battles recorded with weather off.

## S-WEATHER-SIM: the region's weather over time [RE + ASSUMED]

Finding: [S-WEATHER-region-simulation.md](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-WEATHER-region-simulation.md). Code: `WeatherRules`, `Environment/WeatherPattern`.

The game visits the region weather states in turn, `regions_per_update` (7, `common/weather.txt`) an hour, so each region once every `states / 7` hours (TFB 45). At a visit after the current weather has run its duration it rolls (0xf20ef0):
```
period = the first of the region's periods whose between = { d.m d.m } holds the date (no interpolation)
T      = the region's temperature (cell mean; the engine takes the period's mid-range [ASSUMED])
below snowing.temperature's first value (1 °C) no rain; snow and blizzard × (1 − t), t = (clamp(T, −4, 1) + 4) / 5
x      = U1 × (no_phenomenon + rain_light + rain_heavy + snow + blizzard)   (mud, arctic water, sandstorm not in the sum)
         first cumulative weight ≥ x in the order light rain, heavy rain, snow, blizzard; else no phenomenon
arctic water: U1 < arctic_water × clamp(1 − (T − a) / (b − a), 0, 1), (a, b) = arctic_water_temperature, or _end_temperature while on
duration = 24 × (min + min(U1, U2) × (max − min)) hours, (min, max) the new weather's settings.duration (days)
```
The same U1 picks the weather and feeds its duration. [LOG] The logged battles' first roll: U1 0.17168 is light rain for 181.8 h, and region 30's next visit came at battle hour 184, as in all 10 runs. After that the game's weather depends on its whole random history (the hash takes a seed that every hourly tick moves), so it cannot be reproduced; `weather: "simulated"` draws the pattern from its own stream (seeded by `weatherSeed`, else by the battle's seed; never the battle's dice): the visit phase uniform, the start weather one roll entered at a uniform point of its spell, then the visits and rolls above. The `settings` of `common/weather.txt` are loaded with the defines (`WeatherSettings`; TFB has none, so vanilla's apply). With it, 100 simulator seeds of `air_cv_mirror_weather` give the game's means (calibration.md).

## S-TERRAIN: naval terrain [RE]

Finding: [S-TERRAIN-naval-terrain.md](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-TERRAIN-naval-terrain.md). Code: `NavalTerrain`, `CombatStatRules`, `MemberModifierRules.ShipModifier`, `BattleSession.SideModifier`.

The battle's terrain is its strategic region's `naval_terrain` (0x15a5560), or `ocean` when the region sets none (TFB's Black Sea) or names no naval terrain; a scenario's `terrain` overrides it (empty or `region`: the region's). Ocean has no combat effect. A terrain's block (`common/terrain`, `naval_terrain = yes`) has its own keys and per-sub-unit blocks (by the ship type's sub-unit, e.g. `destroyer`) with modifiers and a `units = { attack defence movement }` adjuster; the game merges the terrain's own keys into each sub-unit block at load (0x143e980).
- **Attack and armor** (0xc34a50): Σ += the sub-unit adjuster's `attack` (armor: `defence`), with or without an admiral, so the target's armor takes it too; for attacks the commanding admiral's traits' adjuster for that terrain (0xc19c40; `blue_water_expert`'s `water_deep_ocean`). TFB fjords: −0.2 for battleships, battlecruisers, heavy cruisers, super-heavies and carriers; deep ocean: destroyers −0.2, light cruisers −0.1.
- **Combat speed** (0xc3a740): `speed × max(0.1, 1 + naval_speed_factor + adjuster.movement)`, so also the hit profile and the escape speed. TFB deep ocean: submarines −0.25.
- **Ship modifier sum** (0xc38760): the terrain's own keys plus its block for the ship's sub-unit, e.g. `navy_visibility` in the visibility getters (hit profile, submarine reveal): TFB fjords −0.2 for every ship, shallow sea submarines +1.0, deep ocean submarines −0.15.
- **Side reads** (0x15c70b0): the terrain's own keys whole: `positioning` (TFB fjords −0.15, shallow sea −0.05), screening efficiency.
- Not read in battle: `naval_mine_hit_chance`, `minimum_seazone_dominance`, `navy_fuel_consumption_factor` (fuel is not simulated). The admirals' `movement` terrain adjuster has no combat reader (S-ADMIRAL-ADJUST).

## S-DMG: damage of a hit [RE]

Per slot and target the game computes, once before its shots:
- heavy: `heavyAttack × pierce(heavyPiercing, armor)`; nothing against submarines;
- light: `lightAttack × pierce(lightPiercing, armor)`; against a submarine: `depthChargeAttack × DEPTH_CHARGES_DAMAGE_MULT (0.7) × pierce(lightPiercing, subArmor)`. Depth charges **do** go through armor;
- torpedo: `torpedoAttack`, no armor; later `× (1 - torpedo damage reduction)`;
- the attack and armor above are the combat values of S-STATS-COMBAT: `attack × max(0, 1 + shooter naval_damage_factor)` and `armor × max(0, 1 + target naval_defense_factor)`;
- `base = max(0, damage × max(0, 1 - positioningPenalty + shooter naval_damage_factor - target naval_defense_factor))`. The modifiers come from experience (S-XP); admirals and doctrines would add to them. Each factor therefore counts twice [RE + CAL]: with TFB's green malus (`naval_damage_factor -0.1` only) every logged in-game hit is `profile × 0.9 × 0.9 × randomness`, the profile already including one 0.9 ([LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)). In vanilla the green malus also has `naval_defense_factor -0.1`, so between two green fleets the hit multiplier terms cancel but attack and armor are each ×0.9.

Then per hit: `d = base × (1 + COMBAT_DAMAGE_RANDOMNESS (0.5) × (U - 0.05))` (range ×0.975 to ×1.475); crit roll (S-CRIT); torpedo reduction; split into HP and organization (S-DMG-APPLY); critical multiplier on both.

- **S-DMG-PIERCE**: `ratio = piercing / armor` (armor 0 counts as ratio 100); the first `i` with `ratio >= NAVY_PIERCING_THRESHOLDS[i]` ({2, 1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.1, 0}) selects `NAVY_PIERCING_THRESHOLD_DAMAGE_VALUES[i]` ({1, 1, 0.7, 0.4, 0.3, 0.1}) and `..._CRITICAL_VALUES[i]` ({2, 1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.1, 0}). If nothing matches (possible only with modded tables) the value is `table[0] + 1`.
- **S-DMG-RANDOM**: `1 + R × (U - 0.05)` is confirmed (`DamageRandomnessModel.Game`). The define comment's "7–10" reading is wrong for 1.19.3.
- `COMBAT_ARMOR_PIERCING_DAMAGE_REDUCTION` (0, "deprecated") is still read; not modelled.

**Code.** `DamageRules.Profile` (`CombatStatRules.CombatValue`, `CombatArmor`, `DamageMultiplier`), `RollHit`.

## S-CRIT: critical hits [RE]

- Guns and depth charges: chance `COMBAT_BASE_CRITICAL_CHANCE (0.05) × (1 - reliability) × CRITICAL_VALUES[tier]`; multiplier `1 + COMBAT_CRITICAL_DAMAGE_MULT (5) × (1 - reliability)`. At reliability 0.8 that is ×2.0, not the ×1.8 the old spec had.
- Torpedoes: chance `COMBAT_TORPEDO_CRITICAL_CHANCE (0.1) × max(0, 1 + target torpedo-crit modifier)`; multiplier `COMBAT_TORPEDO_CRITICAL_DAMAGE_MULT` (2), no reliability.
- Reliability is the target's, clamped to 1. A crit is rolled only when chance and damage are positive, with `U <= chance`, and it multiplies **both** HP and organization damage.
- All crit chances are also multiplied by `1 + naval_critical_score_chance_factor` of the shooter `+ critical_receive_chance` of the target (modifier 0x18c, `MODIFIER_NAVAL_CRITICAL_RECEIVE_CHANCE_FACTOR`), both read through the member modifier getter, in the shot loop (the logged shot profile does not include it) [RE + CAL]. TFB's `damage_control_1` and `_2` give −0.1 each: in a replayed battle torpedo crits landed for rolls up to 0.785 of the profile's chance and failed from 0.83, i.e. at 0.8 × the chance, and the part roll followed only those ([LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)). The pride of the fleet's own `critical_receive_chance −0.5` joins it (S-POTF-SHIP). `naval_critical_score_chance_factor_against` a country is not modelled.
- **S-CRIT-PART** [RE + CAL]: on a crit, a part is damaged with chance `CHANCE_TO_DAMAGE_PART_ON_CRITICAL_HIT (0.1) × critical_part_damage_chance_mult / reliability` (0x1974511; no roll and always if that is ≥ 1 or reliability is 0; convoys never).
  - **Parts** come from `common/units/critical_parts/*.txt` (`data/critical-parts/`, imported with the ship data). A key a part leaves out takes the constructor's default (0x1019290): `chance` 1, `str_damage_multiplier`/`org_damage_multiplier` 1, `str_damage`/`org_damage` 0, `base_damage_instance` 1, `damage_instance_per_added_module` 0, `max_damage_instance` −1 (unlimited).
  - **Candidates** (0xc31030): the sub-unit's `critical_parts` (ship type), then each module's, in the game's slot order. A part listed by several modules is **one** candidate: the first brings `base_damage_instance` instances, each further module adds `damage_instance_per_added_module`, capped at `max_damage_instance`. A part damaged its number of instances is no longer a candidate; with none left the regular crit multiplier applies.
  - **Slot order** [CAL]: the game walks a design's slots in the order the slot names first appear in `common/units/equipment/*.txt` (files in load order), not the hull's own order. For TFB: anti-air, radar, engine, secondaries, front_1, mid_1, mid_2, rear_1, mid_3, rear_2, armor, front_2, battery, fire control, torpedo. All 26 part picks of 18 logged TFB battles fit it; the hull order put the SHBB's heavy guns and the light cruiser's secondaries on wrong rolls. The import records it per slot (`gameOrder`), and designs list their modules in it.
  - **Pick** (0xc34500): `r = trunc(U × Σ chance)` in fx5, then walk the candidates subtracting each `chance`; the first that takes `r` below 0 is damaged.
  - **Damage**: the part's values **replace** the crit multiplier: `str = str × str_damage_multiplier + str_damage`, `org = org × org_damage_multiplier + org_damage`. TFB's `destroyed_ammo_storage` (chance 0.1) does ×10 to both, `on_fire` org ×2 + 5.
  - **Lasting effects** for the rest of the battle: the stat penalties (S-STATS-PARTS), and each part modifier × count × `(1 + naval_critical_effect_factor)` in the ship modifier sum (S-STATS-COMBAT; [RE] 0xc38b58: that factor is a full, separate read of the sum, so the country's and the admiral's values count, S-ADMIRAL), e.g. `broken_propeller` (`naval_speed_factor −0.9`), `rudder_jammed` (`naval_retreat_chance −0.9`, `naval_speed_factor −0.5`), `on_fire` (`navy_org_factor −0.5`, `naval_morale_factor −0.8`). [CAL] A broken propeller under TFB's `damage_control_1`/`_2` (−0.1 each) leaves `1 − 0.9 × 0.8 = 0.28` of the speed, which the logged escape of a slow CL3 shows. Organization above the new maximum is cut to it at once (0xc3d4a3).
  - Parts are repaired between battles (`Ship.ResetCombatState`). Air attacks' crits (`CHANCE_TO_DAMAGE_PART_ON_CRITICAL_HIT_FROM_AIR`) do not damage parts in the engine.
  - Code: `CriticalPartRules`, `ShipDesign.CriticalPartCandidates`, `Ship.DamagePart`.

**Code.** `DamageRules.Profile`, `RollHit`, `RollPartDamage`.

## S-DMG-APPLY: HP, organization, manpower [RE]

- `strDamage = d × COMBAT_DAMAGE_TO_STR_FACTOR (0.6)`; `orgDamage = d × (1 - hpBeforeShot / maxHp) × COMBAT_DAMAGE_TO_ORG_FACTOR (1)` ([CAL] on every hit of 12 logged TFB battles; nothing else costs organization in combat, [LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)). The wiki's 100 HP / 50 org example (30 HP, then 0, 15, 30 org) is a unit test.
- Before HP is reduced, experience and manpower losses use the **unclamped** `strDamage` (S-XP). The manpower loss ratio `trunc(strDamage / maxHp)` is `× (1 + navy_casualty_on_hit)` and truncated again (0xc3e305; no loss when that is ≤ 0; TFB's Crisis Management −0.2). Then HP and organization are reduced and clamped at 0.
- A ship sinks at 0 HP; it stops being a target and does not fire for the rest of the hour.

**Code.** `DamageRules.Apply`, `ExperienceRules.ApplyDamageLosses`.

## S-RETREAT: retreat decision [RE + CAL]

Only fighting ships, from tick `COMBAT_MIN_DURATION` (8), before they fire:
- Threshold by the task force **aggressiveness**, the game's "Select aggressiveness" setting (its engagement rule): Do Not Engage and Engage At Low Risk `REPAIR_AND_RETURN_PRIO_LOW_COMBAT` (0.9); Engage At Medium Risk `..._MEDIUM_COMBAT` (0.6); Engage At High Risk and Always Engage `..._HIGH_COMBAT` (0.4). Capital ships and carriers multiply it by `CAPITAL_SHIP_COMBAT_RETREAT_MULT` (0.5).
- If `hp / maxHp < threshold`: roll `U <= COMBAT_RETREAT_DECISION_CHANCE (0.2) × (1 + naval_retreat_chance) × (1 + naval_retreat_chance_after_initial_combat)` (the last factor only after `COMBAT_INITIAL_DURATION`), × (1 + the enemy commanding admiral's `naval_enemy_retreat_chance`, from its traits: container B, S-ADMIRAL-MOD; Gentlemanly +0.2), clamped to [0, 1] (0x1975280; a jammed rudder, S-CRIT-PART, takes it to 0.02); success → retreating, no shots this hour. Organization is not used. [CAL] In 12 logged TFB battles 20 % of fighting ship-hours below the threshold started a retreat and none above it ([LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)).
- The task force's **repair priority** (Never repair, Low, Medium, High; save key `repair_mode`) is a different setting despite the `REPAIR_AND_RETURN_PRIO_*_COMBAT` define names. It decides when a task force goes home for repairs and is not read in combat. Scenarios record it per side (`repairPriority`, default High: the 230 task forces left at their defaults in the recorded save all had `repair_mode=3`, with `navy_engagement_rule=2`, the aggressiveness default), and the engine keeps it with the side without using it.
- [CAL] The aggressiveness, not the repair priority, sets the threshold. In a recorded TFB battle (75 vs 75 light cruisers, `data/battle-scenario-la-vs-torp-cl3.json`), both task forces were on Always Engage (`navy_engagement_rule=4`) and Never repair (`repair_mode=0`). The battle ended with all 74 surviving Romanian ships at or below 40% HP (highest 52.8 of 132). That matches 0.4; Never repair under the repair-priority reading would have been 0.9.

**Not modelled** (documented in the finding): the retreat-chance modifiers (`naval_retreat_chance`, the after-initial-combat one, the enemy's); the side-wide retreat order; the **aggressiveness check**, which makes healthy ships of an outmatched task force retreat (it needs a strength score that is not decoded); and the convoy-raiding submarine rule (`SUBMARINE_ESCAPE_RATIOS`). `REPAIR_AND_RETURN_UNIT_DYING_STR` is not read by naval combat.

**Code.** `RetreatRules.TryStartRetreat`. `BattleRules.RetreatEnabled = false` disables retreat (tooling).

## S-RETREAT-SPEED: escaping [RE]

Daily speed, additive:
```
BASE_ESCAPE_SPEED (0.05) + min(tick × ESCAPE_SPEED_PER_COMBAT_DAY (0.015) / 24, MAX_ESCAPE_SPEED_FROM_COMBAT_DURATION (0.5))
+ retreat speed modifier: naval_retreat_speed for warships, sub_retreat_speed for submarines, convoy_retreat_speed for
  convoys; warships also add naval_retreat_speed_after_initial_combat once tick > COMBAT_INITIAL_DURATION (6)
+ speed × SPEED_TO_ESCAPE_SPEED (0.5) / 100
+ submarines: ESCAPE_SPEED_SUB_BASE (0.1), or ESCAPE_SPEED_HIDDEN_SUB (0.35) while hidden
```
Each retreat speed modifier is read through the member modifier getter `0x1972570`, so it is the sum of:
- the country modifiers (S-STATS-COUNTRY), e.g. the `smoke_generator` technology (vanilla and TFB): `naval_retreat_speed = 0.2`;
- the `night` static modifier × darkness (vanilla has only `naval_retreat_speed 0.1`, so only warships get it);
- the region's weather × the ship's weather factor (S-WEATHER; rain +0.05 to +0.15, arctic water −0.05), and the weather's `naval_speed_factor` through the speed term;
- `screening_bonus` × screening for the battle line and carrier line, and `capital_screening_bonus` × combined carrier screening for the carrier line.

Each hour a retreating ship adds `speed / 24` to its progress, an integer division of the fx5 value (0x19738fd: 40625 raw a day adds 1692 raw an hour), and escapes at 1; [CAL] without the truncation a free-running constant-roll battle (S-REPLAY-CONSTANT) drifted 1e-5 an hour from the game. A 30-knot capital with full screening at tick 10 escapes in about 59 hours.

The modifier ids are mapped to names with medium confidence, except `naval_retreat_speed` (0x5b), which is confirmed: [CAL] in the CL3 battle both countries had `smoke_generator`, and the logged escape progress of every retreating cruiser grew by exactly 0.2 a day more than the formula without it ([LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)). With the country modifier, 200 simulator seeds escape in the same time as the 12 game seeds for every decision hour (decided before hour 100: 36.0 h against the game's 36.1; 100–200: 33.7 and 33.7; 200–300: 31.7 and 31.7; later: 30 and 30).

**Code.** `RetreatRules.EscapeSpeedPerDay`, `AdvanceRetreat`, `MemberModifierRules.Get`; country modifiers in `BattleParticipant.CountryModifiers` and `Ship.CountryModifiers`.

## S-SUB: submarines [RE]

- Submarines start **hidden** (reveal timer 0) and can only be targeted while revealed. Hidden submarines are not counted for activation or line counts.
- At the start of each of its updates, a revealed submarine's timer counts down. If it is hidden and the enemy's detection `D > 0`, it is revealed for `SUBMARINE_HIDE_TIMEOUT` (8) hours when `U <= chance`. The same roll happens after it fires torpedoes, with the numerator × `SUBMARINE_REVEAL_TORPEDO_FIRING_DETECTION_MULTIPLIER` (1.1) and `V × (1 + naval_torpedo_reveal_chance_factor)` (the submarine's; Selective Engagement −0.1, doctrine milestones).
- `chance = min(1, D × SUBMARINE_REVEAL_DETECTION_MULTIPLIER (0.065) / max(SUBMARINE_BASE_STEALTH_VALUE (100) - V, 0.1))`, `V = subVisibility × (1 + navy_visibility) × (1 + SUBMARINE_REVEAL_ON_MINIMUM_POSITIONING (1) × (1 - ownPositioning))`.
- `D` = mean sub detection over the enemy side's ships in battle that have any, × `NAVAL_COMBAT_SUB_DETECTION_FACTOR` (plus an air term, milestone 2).
- A side that faces only hidden submarines disengages (S-HOUR).

**Code.** `SubmarineRules.cs`. **Not modelled:** the sub-only-battle rule at hour 0 (`SUBMARINE_REVEALED_TIMEOUT`). The old `SUBMARINE_REVEAL_BASE_CHANCE`/`POW` defines are not read by 1.19.3.

## S-XP: experience [RE]

- **Levels**: `level = count(NMilitary.UNIT_EXP_LEVELS {0.1, 0.3, 0.75, 0.9} <= xp / NAVY_MAX_XP)`, i.e. 0–4. The level modifiers use the ship's experience at the start of the combat hour, so experience lost to hits during the hour changes them only from the next hour [CAL: in a logged TFB battle the attack in all 8,572 shot profiles matched the shooter's start-of-hour level, while the end-of-hour level differed 185 times, [LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)].
- **Effects**: level `ARMY_EXP_BASE_LEVEL` (1) has none. Below it, the static modifier `ship_experience_malus_min` (`naval_damage_factor -0.1`, `naval_defense_factor -0.1`) × `(base - level) / base`. Above it, `ship_experience_bonus_max` (`+0.3`, `+0.2`) × `(level - base) / (4 - base)`. Damage and defense factors enter the additive damage multiplier (S-DMG). Experience has no other combat effect; carriers get `carrier_experience_*` sortie modifiers instead (air).
- **Gain**: each hour, a ship that fired gains `UNIT_EXPERIENCE_PER_COMBAT_HOUR (10) × UNIT_EXPERIENCE_SCALE (1) × (enemyGroupSize + 1) / (ownGroupSize + 1) × EXPERIENCE_FACTOR_NON_CARRIER_GAIN (0.04)`, × `EXPERIENCE_FACTOR_CONVOY_ATTACK` (0.04) if its last target was a convoy. Damage dealt does not matter. Group size counts every ship the side brought (sunk ships stay in the group's list; medium confidence). Carriers that did not fire gain from sortie efficiency × `EXPERIENCE_FACTOR_CARRIER_GAIN` (air). `XP_GAIN_FACTOR` is not used here.
- **S-XP-GAIN** [RE] (0xc3b240, [finding](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-POTF-pride-of-the-fleet.md#the-experience-add-0xc3b240-and-its-caller-0xc31840)): either gain is added as `(gain + experience_gain_navy_unit) × (1 + experience_gain_navy_unit_factor + experience_gain_<sub-unit>_combat_factor)` (fx15, floored), with the country's values, plus the `pride_of_the_fleet` block for the pride of the fleet (factor +0.25). No admiral term. The ship's medals (the "captain" record at `ship+0x8f8`, vanilla +0.05 factor each) are not modelled. Code: `ExperienceRules.ModifiedGain`.
- **Loss** on each hit, before HP drops: `xp × clamp(hp - EXPERIENCE_LOSS_FACTOR (1) × strDamage, 0, maxHp) / hp`. Manpower loss: `round(maxManpower × trunc5(strDamage / maxHp) × MANPOWER_LOSS_RATIO_ON_STR_LOSS (0.5))`, the ratio truncated to fx5 [CAL: rounding the exact ratio misses 48 of 22087 logged losses, the truncated ratio none], never below `round(maxManpower × MIN_MANPOWER_RATIO_TO_DROP (0.1))`, and nothing once at that floor. Rounding is half away from zero.

**Code.** `ExperienceRules.cs`.

## S-MANPOWER: reinforcement during battle [RE + CAL]

Ships regain manpower **once a day, in combat too**, despite the define's comment ("ships not in combat"). The game's daily update (`gamestate.daily` → `country.daily` → `fleets.daily` → `CTaskForce::DailyUpdate`) refills every task force without checking for combat:
- `gain = min(maxManpower - manpower, ceil(maxManpower × DAILY_MANPOWER_GAIN_RATIO (0.05)))`, the ceiling taken in fx5; 60 a day for a 1200-crew ship.
- **S-STATS-MANPOWER** [PDX + LOG]: a ship's crew (`maxManpower`) is the hull's `manpower` plus each module's `manpower` (written outside `add_stats`; deck spaces 500, large deck spaces 800 in vanilla and TFB). A carrier with four deck spaces has 7,500, not the hull's 5,500: in game, carriers written with 5,500 were raised to 7,500 at the first daily update. Imported since 2026-10-20 (`modules/*.json` `manpower`).
- It runs after the combat hour of the tick that starts a new day (00:00), so after battle hour `h` with `(startHourOfDay + h) % 24 == 0`.
- Every ship afloat refills, including escaped ones (still in their task force).
- **A sinking refills the task force too** [RE + CAL]: the combat's sink handler `0x1975480` (which sets the member's state to sunk) calls the task force's remove-ship function `0xd77ff0` (at 0x1975879), and that ends with the same refill `0xd7a7e0(taskForce, force = false)` (at 0xd78b71), which refills every ship once the game has started. So every sinking gives the rest of the sunk ship's task force the daily gain at once, in the middle of the hour; in a replayed battle every manpower rise outside 00:00 came with a sinking, and later shots that hour saw the new crews ([LOG](../reverse-engineering/findings/LOG-ingame-logging.md)).
- The manpower is drawn from the country's pool. [ASSUMED] The pool is never short. Before the refill, `0xd77ff0` returns `round(manpower × (1 − clamp(MANPOWER_LOSS_RATIO_ON_SUNK (0.5) × (1 + navy_casualty_on_sink), 0, 1)))` of the sunk ship's crew to the pool; the strategic-region static modifier `water_modifier_shark_infested` (`navy_casualty_on_sink = 0.6`, Pacific regions in vanilla and TFB) makes that 20 % instead of 50 %. It only matters to the pool, so the engine does not model it.

Calibration: in the CL3 battle (`battle-scenario-la-vs-torp-cl3.json`) the save from the battle's last hour had 74 of 75 BUL and 71 of 74 ROM ships at full manpower after losing 25% and 72% of their HP. Without the refill the simulator left almost every ship short; with it, 74.5 and 70.1 on average (200 seeds).

**Code.** `ExperienceRules.DailyManpowerGain`, `BattleSession.ApplyDailyManpowerGain`, `BattleSession.ReinforceAfterSinking`. Manpower matters in combat through the crew factor in hit chance (S-HIT-MODS).

## S-RECOVERY: ship recovery chance [RE + LOG]

From [S-RECOVERY](../reverse-engineering/findings/S-RECOVERY-ship-recovery-chance.md). When a ship sinks, the task force's remove-ship function `0xd77ff0` (called by the sink handler) first rolls whether the ship is recovered instead:
- `chance` = the task force's support entries (each support type's static modifier, e.g. `naval_repair_support`'s `naval_ship_recovery_chance = 0.1`, × its support ratio; not modelled: the simulator has no support ships) + the owner country's `naval_ship_recovery_chance` (spirits, Japan's focus via its dynamic modifier) + `NAVAL_DOMINANCE_SHIP_RECOVERY_CHANCE` (0.1) only if the country controlling the task force's **province** has naval dominance in the region; a sea province has no controller, so this never applies at sea [LOG: two ROM ships sunk while ROM controlled the region got no bonus].
- If `chance > 0` the game draws the roll (an integer draw of the battle's generator, taskforce.cpp line 0xb31, bit-mixed into 0..99999; `RollKind.ShipRecovery`).
- `final = trunc(chance × naval_ship_recovery_chance_factor)`: **the factor itself, not 1 + it.** No game or TFB script sets that factor, so `final` is 0 for every country and **no ship is ever recovered in 1.19.3** [LOG: the factor read 0 at all 71 probed sinkings; with the chance forced to 0.5 in game, none of 28 sunk ships was recovered].
- Recovered if `roll < final`. The ship's HP becomes `SUPPORT_SHIP_RECOVERY_BASE_STRENGTH_FACTOR (0.01) × the summed maximum HP of the ships still in its task force` (the ship included), at most its own maximum: in a large task force a recovered ship comes back almost undamaged, not at 1 % (the define's comment says the ship's own maximum; the code reads the task force's) [LOG: 18 of 18 recoveries with the factor forced to 1]. The simulator takes a side as one task force.
- A recovered ship is out of the battle like a sunk one (its member state stays sunk; it neither fires nor is targeted), keeps its crew, and its task force gets **no** refill (the function returns before `0xd7a7e0`, S-MANPOWER). It leaves the task force (`0xd72580`) and can fight again in a later battle (outside the simulator). Results list it as recovered, not sunk.

**Code.** `RecoveryRules`, `BattleSession.LeaveBySinking`, `AirPhase.Strike` (the roll inside an air strike, before its ace rolls). With the game's content (factor 0) the engine never recovers a ship either, but it makes the roll whenever the chance before the factor is above 0, as the game does.

## S-REPLAY: replaying a logged game battle [tooling]

`tools/ingame/battle_logger.js` records, for every combat hour, each member's state at the start (after the daily update) and the end of the hour, every `random_fixed` call of the combat update on its thread in call order with its source line, and every shot. `NavySimulator replay --log <run>` (`GameData/Replay/GameBattleLog`, `Engine/Battles/Replay/BattleReplay`) builds the scenario with the game's attacker, orders each fleet like the game's members, and fights each hour from the game's start state with the game's rolls (`ReplayRandomSource`): every engine draw names its `RollKind`, which must be the kind of the game's next roll. Line tags (hoi4.exe 1.19.3): 0x37b target pick, 0x3d9 torpedo line, 0x587 hit, 0x593 damage randomness, 0x59f crit, 0x2f4 part damage, 0x78f part pick (skipped only for data without parts), 0xa83 retreat decision, 0x40e the 6–9 rolls of a sinking (skipped). The game's logged hit chance is compared with the engine's (tolerance 2e-5). With parts, 11 replayed `jukki_usa_vs_bc_japan` battles (seeds 1 and 18–27, 120,090 shots) match every shot, and each side's HP damage within 0.1 HP; what differs is organization regain once the game hands naval dominance to Japan (S-DOMINANCE-DAILY; without `rules.navalDominanceEnabled`, all of it), in the last hour the game skipping the end-of-hour regain and experience once the battle has ended, and 4 manpower values off by one (the engine's damage is not computed in fx5). Shots are compared one by one (shooter, weapon, target, hit, HP and organization lost) and end-of-hour states against the next hour's start. Setting the state every hour keeps fixed-point rounding from accumulating.

**Air** (since 2026-10-20). The logger also records the generator's integer draws (the air target pick, line 0x43f; 0x96 with sinkings, 0x1c9 and 0xb72 bookkeeping), each air object's strike (sortie efficiency, target, interception, AA kills, the stat averages, the damage it applied), every wing's planes, disruption, mission effectiveness and ace, the carrier-ratio helper's sums, and every air object's wings at the end of the hour. The replay sets each hour's wing planes, aces, disruption and counted planes from the log (the region's air war, A-REGION, happens outside the logged combat update and is turned off), maps air objects by the game's pointer and wings by their handles (the region's air war deletes wings), and compares strike by strike (target, planes shot down, HP and organization damage) and the side values (positioning, screening, fleet AA). `--design <logged name>=<design id>` maps a logged variant name to a renamed design (docs/calibration.md). `--region <file>` also runs the engine's region air war as a dry run from the game's wings at the start of each air hour and writes, per wing, the engine's and the game's disruption and planes lost. Roll lines: 0x27d interception, 0x212 / 0x21c AA chance and kills, 0x45d hits, 0x487 crit, 0x2f4 / 0x78f part, 0xb68 ace earned, 0xb52 ace death. In 40 replayed TFB carrier battles every roll followed the game's in most hours; the strikes that differ are mostly fixed-point rounding of the disrupted efficiency, and in a few hours an ace that moved between wings.

**S-REPLAY-CONSTANT: one constant roll** (2026-10-26). `run_battles.py --constant-roll U` makes every roll of the naval combat update that the engine also makes (lines 0x37b, 0x3d9, 0x587, 0x593, 0x59f, 0x2f4, 0x78f, 0xa83) return U, by overwriting `random_fixed`'s result on the combat thread (`battle_logger.js`); the sinking rolls (0x40e) and integer draws keep the generator's values. `NavySimulator replay --log <run> --constant-roll U` then fights the battle free (`ConstantRandomSource`): the game's attacker, fleet order and first-hour state, and from then on nothing from the log, so each hour compares the whole battle so far and the end hour is checked. `NavySimulator --constant-roll U` runs a scenario with the constant. With no roll sequence to keep in step, the same rules from the same start must give the same battle. The order of the members matters: with one value the target pick always takes the same point of the weights, and the game puts each side's super-heavy battleships first (SHBB, BC, DD and SHBB, BB, CL in `jukki_usa_vs_bc_japan_battle_start`), where the scenario's fleets list designs by ID, so a scenario run is a different battle (43 h instead of 137). With random rolls the order changes no odds. Air (2026-10-26): the logger also forces the air strike's rolls (lines 0x27d, 0x212, 0x21c, 0x45d, 0x487, 0xb68, 0xb52), the air target pick's integer draw (0x43f, to `floor(U × 2^31)`, which `ConstantRandomSource` returns for `AirTargetPick`), and outside the combat update, by call site, the region air war's rolls (0xf862a0: losses 0xacf, ace credit 0xade / 0xae5; 0xf7be70: ace earn 0x82b / 0x833 / 0x834; 0xc5f4c0: the fight's province, integer 0x12c9); the free replay runs the region air war from its second hour (the first hour's logged state already holds the game's). The accident pass is off in both (the logger replaces 0xc60290 with a no-op; `AirPhase.AccidentsEnabled`): its local generator is seeded once an hour and shared by every air base in the world (A-ACCIDENT), so the engine cannot know where our bases fall in it. U must stay above `ACE_EARN_CHANCE_BASE`: a new ace's template comes from the game's own hash (A-ACE-CREATE). Runbook: tools/ingame/README.md. First air runs (the four air saves at U 0.05 and 0.07, `output/ingame/constant_roll_air/`): the land-plane battle and the carrier mirror match every shot and every strike to the end; bombers against fighters all shots but one and all strikes but one; they found the preemptive AA chance needing fx5 (A-AA-PREEMPT) and the carrier sortie experience's stance window (air.md). In `air_cv_land_mix` the logger's new per-mission fields (disruption taken and escort) showed the escort sums counting carrier wings on both lists twice (A-REGION; fixed): hours 1 and 9 now match. From the second day the USA land wings' effectiveness at Burgas follows the base's crowding, and the template's other BUL planes there change at the day change (the game's logged effectiveness implies 1000, 1038, 1019, 1000 other planes at hours 9, 17, 25, 33; the engine keeps `otherPlanes` 1000), so the USA's region attack differs from hour 17 on. Open: model or remove those planes (the test template's, not a combat rule). First run, the mixed fleets with U = 0.05: all 6,164 shots match, both sides' damage exactly (11,824.0 and 9,082.3 HP), the engine's battle ends in the game's last hour (135), and the end-of-hour states match but for the known last-hour regain and experience and three fleet AA values off by 3e-4; it found the escape progress truncation (S-RETREAT-SPEED).

## S-END: end of battle [RE]

The battle ends when either side has no ship left in it (waiting, fighting or retreating with HP above 0). Retreating ships keep the battle going until they escape. There is no maximum duration in the game; `BattleRules.MaxHours` is a safety stop (`Timeout`). `ContinueAfterRetreat` (tooling) puts escaped ships back in.
